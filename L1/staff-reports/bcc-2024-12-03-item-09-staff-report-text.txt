CURRENT SITUATION AND ITS EFFECTS
Ohlone Park is a five-block linear park that hosts a variety of recreational amenities,
including but not limited to a softball field, playgrounds, volleyball court, basketball court,
grass fields, dog park, community garden, and outdoor exercise equipment. It is popular
amongst people of all ages and is heavily used, but the only restroom facilities currently
available at this park are porta-potties. In addition to recreational users, the pathways
are used by commuters as Ohlone Park provides a connection to North Berkeley BART.

This project includes a new pre-fabricated, two-stall restroom located in the block
between California Street and McGee Avenue, between the Ohlone soccer field and
volleyball court. This project also includes the addition of dark sky-compliant light
fixtures in existing dark spots along the Ohlone pathway.

The Ohlone Park Restroom and Lighting Project is funded by Measure T1, the 2016
voter-approved bond to pay for improvements to City infrastructure and facilities.

BACKGROUND
The project was advertised for bids on Friday, September 6, 2024, and bids were
opened on Tuesday, October 17, 2024. The City received 5 bids, from a low base bid of
$698,000 to a high base bid of $1,012,133. With the exception of the lowest bid,
all bids were above the amount budgeted for this project. The determination of the
lowest responsive and responsible bidder was based on the price for base bid work, as
indicated in the bid documents.

In the evaluation and review of construction bids, the City considers the Bidder’s
qualifications, and conducts reasonable investigations and reference checks of the
Bidder and other persons and organizations as the City deems necessary to assist in
the evaluation of any bid, and to establish the Bidder’s responsibility, qualifications, and
ability to perform the Work in accordance with our contract documents. The City
reserves the right to consider information provided by sources other than the Bidder. If
the City believes that it would not be in the best interest of the Project to make an award
to that Bidder, or the Bidder fails to meet other pertinent standards or criteria
established by the City, the City may reject the Bid as non-responsive.



The lowest bidder is Azul Works, Inc. (Azul). In reviewing Azul Works’ references and
qualifications, it was discovered that Azul was involved in a case that resulted in a
federal criminal complaint with the City of San Francisco’s former Public Works Director
that surfaced in 2020. The City of San Francisco currently has a pending request to
debar the ex-CEO of Azul, Balmore Hernandez, and his former company, from being
awarded City of San Francisco projects. This request has been pending with an order
to stay debarment proceedings pending resolution of the federal criminal complaint. At
this point, neither Balmore Hernandez nor his former company Azul Works, Inc. have
been disbarred.

Upon review, Azul has no previous history working with the City of Berkeley and their
references checks, including recently completed projects with other public agencies,
were satisfactory. Additionally, Azul’s submittal met all City requirements.

Based on these findings and that the City is required to award to the lowest responsive
and responsible bidder, Staff recommends that it would be in the City’s best interest to
award the construction contract to Azul Works, Inc.

The Living Wage Ordinance does not apply to this project since construction contracts
are, pursuant to City policy, subject to State prevailing wage laws. The contractor will
need to submit a certification of Compliance for the Equal Benefits Ordinance. The
contract will be subject to the Community Workforce Agreement.

ENVIRONMENTAL SUSTAINABILITY AND CLIMATE IMPACTS
The construction contract includes requirements to comply with the City’s
Environmentally Preferable Purchasing Policy and Construction and Demolition Waste
program of at least 50%.

RATIONALE FOR RECOMMENDATION
The new pre-fabricated restroom and light fixtures are needed as part of the City’s
ongoing program to repair, renovate and improve accessibility at non-compliant or aging
Parks facilities. The City does not have in-house labor or equipment resources to
complete this renovation project.

ALTERNATIVE ACTIONS CONSIDERED
None

WHEREAS, the Ohlone Park Restroom and Lighting Project is a Measure T1 project; and
