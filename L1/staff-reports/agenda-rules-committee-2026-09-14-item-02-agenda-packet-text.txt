Rules of Procedure as adopted by Council resolution, Article III, C3c - Agenda - Submission of Time Critical
Items
Time Critical Items. A Time Critical item is defined as a matter that is considered urgent by the sponsor
and that has a deadline for action that is prior to the next meeting of the Council and for which a report
prepared by the City Manager, Auditor, Mayor or council member is received by the City Clerk after
established deadlines and is not included on the Agenda Committee’s published agenda.
If the Agenda Committee finds the matter to meet the definition of Time Critical, the Agenda Committee
may place the matter on the Agenda on either the Consent or Action Calendar.
The City Clerk shall not accept any item past the adjournment of the Agenda Committee meeting for which
the agenda that the item is requested to appear on has been approved.
Written communications addressed to the Agenda Committee and submitted to the City Clerk Department
by 5:00 p.m. the Friday before the Committee meeting, will be distributed to the Committee in advance of
the meeting and retained as part of the official record.
This meeting will be conducted in accordance with the Brown Act, Government Code Section 54953 and
applicable Executive Orders as issued by the Governor that are currently in effect. Members of the City
Council who are not members of the standing committee may attend a standing committee meeting even
if it results in a quorum being present, provided that the non-members only act as observers and do not
participate in the meeting. If only one member of the Council who is not a member of the committee is
present for the meeting, the member may participate in the meeting because less than a quorum of the
full Council is present. Any member of the public may attend this meeting. Questions regarding public
participation may be addressed to the City Clerk Department (510) 981-6900.

 Land Acknowledgement Statement: The City of Berkeley recognizes that the community we
 live in was built on the territory of xučyun (Huchiun (Hooch-yoon)), the ancestral and unceded land of the
 Chochenyo (Cho-chen-yo)-speaking Ohlone (Oh-low-nee) people, the ancestors and descendants of the
 sovereign Verona Band of Alameda County. This land was and continues to be of great importance to all
 of the Ohlone Tribes and descendants of the Verona Band. As we begin our meeting tonight, we
 acknowledge and honor the original inhabitants of Berkeley, the documented 5,000-year history of a
 vibrant community at the West Berkeley Shellmound, and the Ohlone people who continue to reside in
 the East Bay. We recognize that Berkeley’s residents have and continue to benefit from the use and
 occupation of this unceded stolen land since the City of Berkeley’s incorporation in 1878. As stewards of
 the laws regulating the City of Berkeley, it is not only vital that we recognize the history of this land, but
 also recognize that the Ohlone people are present members of Berkeley and other East Bay communities
 today. The City of Berkeley will continue to build relationships with the Lisjan Tribe and to create
 meaningful actions that uphold the intention of this land acknowledgement.

 Consent Calendar
     The Council will first determine whether to move items on the agenda for “Action” or “Information” to the
     “Consent Calendar”, or move “Consent Calendar” items to “Action.” Three members of the City Council
     must agree to pull an item from the Consent Calendar or Information Calendar for it to move to Action.
     Items that remain on the “Consent Calendar” are voted on in one motion as a group. “Information” items
     are not discussed or acted upon at the Council meeting unless they are moved to “Action” or “Consent”.
     No additional items can be moved onto the Consent Calendar once public comment has commenced. At
     any time during, or immediately after, public comment on Information and Consent items, any
     Councilmember may move any Information or Consent item to “Action.” Following this, the Council will
     vote on the items remaining on the Consent Calendar in one motion.
     For items moved to the Action Calendar from the Consent Calendar or Information Calendar, persons
     who spoke on the item during the Consent Calendar public comment period may speak again at the time
     the matter is taken up during the Action Calendar.

     Public Comment on Consent Calendar and Information Items Only: The Council will
     take public comment on any items that are either on the amended Consent Calendar or the Information
     Calendar. If ten or fewer persons are interested in speaking on an individual agenda item, each speaker
     may speak for two minutes. If there are more than ten persons interested in speaking, the Presiding
     Officer may limit the public comment for all speakers to one minute per speaker. Speakers are permitted
     to yield their time to one other speaker, however no one speaker shall have more than four minutes. The
     Presiding Officer may, with the consent of persons representing both sides of an issue, allocate a block of
     time to each side to present their issue. A speaker may only speak once during the period for public
     comment on Consent Calendar and Information items.

     Additional information regarding public comment by City of Berkeley employees and interns: Employees
     and interns of the City of Berkeley, although not required, are encouraged to identify themselves as such,
     the department in which they work and state whether they are speaking as an individual or in their official
     capacity when addressing the Council in open session or workshops.

RECOMMENDATION
Adopt a Resolution approving an expenditure of an amount not to exceed $1,000 per

Council, including contributions of $500 from Councilmember Igor Tregub, $250 from
Councilmember Terry Taplin, $100 from Mayor Ishii, and $100 from Councilmember

Mokhtar Paki’s “Sinbad Voyage” mural.

BACKGROUND
“Sinbad Voyage” is a large-scale public mural created by Berkeley-based Iranian
immigrant artist Mokhtar Paki in 2023. Located at the corner of Dwight Way and
Sacramento Street, the 28-by-14-foot mural depicts the experiences, journeys, and
resilience of immigrants, refugees, and displaced people from around the world.

The mural was inspired in part by the story of Sinbad’s voyage, which follows a traveler
journeying with others in search of a better life, and by the real-world refugee and
migration crises that have affected millions of people across Africa, Asia, the Middle
East, and elsewhere. Mr. Paki began developing the concept years before the mural
was painted, drawing inspiration from the stories of people who risked their lives
seeking safety and a better future for themselves and their families.




                                                                                  Page 23
                                        Page 2 of 5




Mokhtar has described “Sinbad Voyage” as his “love letter to Berkeley.” The mural
reflects Berkeley’s history as a community shaped by immigrants and people from
diverse backgrounds, faiths, cultures, and countries of origin. It also reflects the deeply
personal nature of migration: the mural includes more than 450 figures, including
approximately 90 whose faces are clearly visible, many of whom represent actual
people and real immigrant stories.

Among the figures depicted are individuals whose stories illustrate the profound human
cost of displacement and migration, including Mokhtar’s own young niece, Sanaz, who
died at five years old while attempting to flee Iran by boat with her father. The mural
also depicts refugees and migrants from Central America, the Middle East, Africa, and
other parts of the world, as well as members of Berkeley’s own diverse communities.

In this way, “Sinbad Voyage” is more than a mural. It is a public expression of memory,
migration, loss, resilience, and belonging, and a reflection of the many stories that make
Berkeley the community it is today.

CURRENT SITUATION AND ITS EFFECTS
On the night of August 19, 2026, “Sinbad Voyage” was vandalized with white paint,
causing significant damage to the mural.

The vandalism has deeply affected the artist and members of the Berkeley community
who see their own experiences, histories, and identities reflected in the work. In the
days following the incident, community members gathered around the mural, left
messages of solidarity, and expressed their desire to help with its restoration.

The Berkeley Police Department is actively investigating this vandalism case.
Investigators have released video showing a person of interest walking through the
neighborhood carrying what appears to be a can of paint and have pursued additional
investigative leads. Police have also reported a related same-day incident in which
white paint was poured onto a vehicle parked approximately one block from the mural.
The investigation remains ongoing.

At this time, the motive for the vandalism has not been established. Nevertheless, the
damage to “Sinbad Voyage” has understandably generated significant concern in a
community that values immigrant stories, cultural diversity, and public art as essential
parts of Berkeley’s identity.

Councilmember Tregub and his staff have met with Mr. Paki and City staff at the mural
to hear directly from the artist and discuss potential avenues for City support for the
restoration efforts. In addition, Councilmember Tregub’s office has been in




                                                                                   Page 24
                                       Page 3 of 5




communication with the Mayor’s Office, the Civic Arts Commission, and community
members regarding ways to restore the mural and assist the artist.

Many local artists and community members have expressed strong interest in helping
with the restoration process, including through potential volunteer events and other
forms of in-kind assistance. The Civic Arts Commission is exploring opportunities for
support, while City staff continue to identify potential funding sources and appropriate
mechanisms for assisting with restoration.

Mr. Paki has also established a community fundraising campaign to help cover the cost
of restoring “Sinbad Voyage.” The campaign emphasizes that restoration will require
financial resources as well as community support and calls upon people who value
public art, immigrant stories, and community healing to participate in the effort.

The City Council’s support through D13 discretionary funds would provide an immediate
contribution toward restoration while also demonstrating the City’s commitment to
protecting and preserving public art that reflects the experiences and contributions of
Berkeley’s diverse communities.

FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS
There is no impact to the General Fund. Contributions of up to $1000 per participating
members of the Berkeley City Council will come from existing Council Office Budget
discretionary accounts.

SUBJECT:       Referral to the City Manager to develop an updated Cross Departmental Program
               Plan and Strategic Asset Management Plan per the Realize Vision 2050 Plan to
               address the City’s Long-term Infrastructure Needs and Maintenance Schedule and
               Provide Semi-annual Progress Reports to City Council.

RECOMMENDATION
Refer to the City Manager to develop an updated cross-departmental Program Plan and Strategic
Asset Management Plan as set forth in the Realize Vision 2050 Plan to address the City’s long-
term infrastructure needs and maintenance schedule, and provide semi-annual progress reports to
City Council.

CURRENT SITUATION AND ITS EFFECTS
The City of Berkeley has approximately $1.5 billion in unfunded infrastructure needs. Currently,
the City is taking steps to address this need, but to effectively accomplish this goal, it is
imperative that the City continue to amplify its work to understand the landscape of the need and
have criteria for setting priorities. To accomplish this, the City needs an updated Program Plan
and Strategic Asset Management Plan. A Program Plan is a high-level strategic document that
outlines the City’s long-term organizational goals and coordinates projects across the appropriate
departments. This document will outline all the infrastructure projects Citywide that need to be
completed, attached with a rough cost analysis of each project and a prioritization plan informed
by urgency and the Realize Vision 2050 plan. The Asset Management Plan is a roadmap for
managing the City’s physical assets, including infrastructure, equipment and facilities over the
course of their life cycles. This will work in tandem with the Program Plan to help the
Departments keep track of maintenance plans for the City’s assets.

To promote transparency on how the City is progressing and addressing its infrastructure needs,
City staff should institute a transparency hub dashboard to publicly track its infrastructure
projects. This dashboard could be akin to the Transportation Studio: Capital Improvement



                                                                                        Page 31
                                            Page 2 of 2




Program (CIP) Hub created by the UC Berkeley students in the Transportation Planning Studio
course.1 This Hub will track current project schedules and their budgets, as well as integrate
future capital planning.

The City Manager will provide semi-annual (every six months) progress reports on the Program
Plan and the Strategic Asset Management Plan to the City Council.

BACKGROUND
The City of Berkeley maintains over 300 miles of sidewalks, 54 parks, 95 buildings, as well as
municipal drains, streets and much more.2 In 2020, the City of Berkeley put forth the Vision
2050 plan, which is “a long-term infrastructure plan to create a city that is resilient and
sustainable for future generations.”3 Following the November 16, 2021 Vision 2050 Progress
Report4, on May 10, 2022, the Berkeley City Council approved Vision 2050: Strategic Asset
Management Plan and Asset Management Policy.5 Because infrastructure is one of Mayor
Adena Ishii’s priority areas, she reconvened the Vision 2050 Task Force in 2025 to update the
plan, with consideration to the significant changes that have occurred between 2020 and 2025.6
This new plan, called Realize Vision 2050, iterates the need to implement both an Infrastructure
Program Plan, as well as an Asset Management Plan. Both plans are integral to project
prioritization and creating a comprehensive landscape of City infrastructure needs. Both Vision
2050 and Realize Vision 2050 should be foundational in building out the plans.

ENVIRONMENTAL SUSTAINABILITY
There are no environmental impacts generated by this referral.

FISCAL IMPACTS
The fiscal impact to update the Program Plan and the Strategic Asset Management Plan is
estimated at approximately $200,000, which includes staff and consultant time.

Subject:      Reaffirmation of the Sister City Relationship Between the City of Berkeley
and the City of Palma Soriano, Cuba



RECOMMENDATION
Adopt a Resolution reaffirming the long-standing Sister City relationship between the
City of Berkeley and the City of Palma Soriano, Cuba, and reauthorizing the Berkeley–
Palma Soriano Sister City Committee to resume work on clean water, environmental,
cultural, and educational projects in partnership with the Palma Soriano community, at
no direct or indirect cost to the City of Berkeley.

SUMMARY
On May 14, 2002, the City of Berkeley established a Sister City relationship with the
Municipality of Palma Soriano in the Province of Santiago de Cuba through Resolution
61,553-N.S. In the years since, Berkeley residents and organizations, including the
Berkeley-Cuba Sister City Association, the Green Cities Fund, and the Berkeley–Palma
Soriano Sister City Committee, worked alongside the Palma Soriano community and
Cuban partner organizations to advance a locally designed, low-cost clean water
project. But the Sister City relationship has been in abeyance for an extended period.
This item reaffirms the relationship and reauthorizes the Sister City Committee to
resume its work, consistent with the criteria the City established for Sister City
relationships in Resolution 56,069-N.S.

FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS
None. Consistent with the City's Sister City criteria, there are to be no direct or indirect
costs to the City of Berkeley. All expenses associated with the relationship and its
associated projects are the responsibility of the Berkeley–Palma Soriano Sister City
Committee, the Green Cities Fund, and partnering organizations, which take
responsibility for any related fundraising.

                                                                                     Page 37

Because the clean water project is an international effort rather than a local Berkeley
organization, program, or project, Councilmembers may not contribute to it from their
Council Office Budgets, which are restricted to local purposes. Councilmember Humbert
will instead be making a personal donation in support of the effort and warmly invites his
colleagues and members of the public who are moved to do so to contribute personally
as well, through the Green Cities Fund (www.greencitiesfund.org), a Berkeley-based
501(c)(3) public benefit corporation.

CURRENT SITUATION AND ITS EFFECTS
Palma Soriano is a community of roughly 80,000 people in the Province of Santiago de
Cuba in eastern Cuba, at the confluence of the two main tributaries of the Cauto, Cuba's
longest river, which descend from the Sierra Maestra mountains. Like Berkeley, it is
known as a center for research, social programs, and culture. The community has a rich
Afro-Cuban heritage, including descendants of people brought from Haiti more than two
centuries ago in the era of the Haitian Revolution.

For years, raw sewage in Palma Soriano has been discharged directly into the Cauto
River, the community's own water source. Working together, University of California,
Berkeley clean water experts, the Berkeley–Palma Soriano Sister City organization,
Cuba's leading environmental non-governmental organization (the Antonio Núñez
Jiménez Foundation), and Palma Soriano's independent cultural organization (Grupo
Ennegro) developed a low-technology, low-cost, and locally built clean water plan.
Rather than emptying into the river, sewage would be directed into a series of ponds
filled with flora and fauna that naturally remove toxins and sediment, yielding clean
water that can be used for reforestation and community gardens. The approach is
designed to be replicable by community groups elsewhere in Cuba and other
developing regions.

The plan was devised with the assistance of University of California, Berkeley faculty as
a graduate thesis project. As a next step, the Green Cities Fund is prepared to finance a
return visit by the expert who designed the plan to provide an updated assessment of
feasibility and cost. Reaffirming the Sister City relationship and reauthorizing the Sister
City Committee will bolster this work moving forward and will renew opportunities for
cultural and educational exchange between our two communities.

The relationship continues to satisfy the seven criteria for Sister City relationships
established in Resolution 56,069-N.S.:

1. Benefit to the human rights, health, safety, culture, and education of the
citizens of Berkeley. The relationship deepens Berkeley residents' engagement with



                                         Page 2 of 5
                                                                                    Page 38

Afro-Cuban history and culture and with community-scale environmental problem-
solving, and reflects Berkeley's long-standing commitment to international friendship
and solidarity.
2. New opportunities for exchange programs, cultural enrichment, or curriculum
enhancement. The partnership creates opportunities for cultural and technical
exchange between Berkeley and Palma Soriano, including around the clean water
project and among the communities' cultural organizations.
3. A sufficient number of committed Berkeley residents capable of representing
Berkeley's interest. Berkeley residents and organizations, including the Berkeley-Cuba
Sister City Association that originally proposed the relationship, former Mayor Tom
Bates, former State Senator and Mayor Loni Hancock, the Wellstone Democratic Club,
the Green Cities Fund, and the Berkeley–Palma Soriano Sister City Committee, have
sustained this relationship over many years, including in-person delegations to Palma
Soriano.
4. A sufficient number of committed residents in the Sister City capable of
sustaining the relationship. Palma Soriano's cultural and environmental
organizations, together with Cuban partner institutions, have demonstrated ongoing
commitment to the Sister City partnership and the clean water project.
6. No direct or indirect costs to the City of Berkeley. All costs are borne by the
Sister City Committee, the Green Cities Fund, and partnering organizations, with any
donations being of a personal nature and not drawing from public funds.
7. Increased harmony in Berkeley and consistency with Berkeley’s best interests.
The relationship reflects Berkeley's historic commitment to standing with communities
seeking to chart their own future and to advancing environmental sustainability and
international friendship.

ENVIRONMENTAL SUSTAINABILITY AND CLIMATE IMPACTS
There are no adverse environmental effects associated with this recommendation. The
clean water project is designed to reduce water pollution, restore a natural watershed,
and support reforestation and community gardens in Palma Soriano.

To:              Members of the Berkeley City Council

From:            Councilmember Mark Humbert (Author);
                 Mayor Adena Ishii (Co-Author);
                 Councilmember Ben Bartlett (Co-Author)

Subject:         Project to Increase Telegraph and Claremont Housing (PITCH) Rezoning

RECOMMENDATION

Refer to the City Manager the Project to Increase Telegraph and Claremont Housing (PITCH)
as a standalone rezoning effort to proactively and equitably increase housing stock in southeast
Berkeley’s high-resource areas. This project would seek to:

      1. Rezone the Telegraph Avenue corridor south of Parker Street to the Oakland border at
         Woolsey Street from the current C-C (Commercial-Corridor) district to the C-T
         (Telegraph Avenue Commercial) district (or similar), allowing for taller mixed-use
         housing development, with a goal of base zoning of 8 stories/85 feet, potentially adjusted
         to optimize production of affordable inclusionary units.
      2. Identify and upzone specific auto-oriented C-N (Neighborhood Commercial) parcels in
         the Claremont and Ashby area (near Domingo Avenue) from their current 2–3 story
         limits to 4–6 stories using existing or new zoning districts.
      3. Allocate funding from the District 8 office budget to begin required planning, General
         Plan amendments, and/or California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) review for the
         proposed zoning changes.

SUMMARY

This item proposes a new, targeted rezoning effort to deliver a substantial and equitable
increase in zoned housing capacity in District 8 (and limited portions of District 3 along
Telegraph). The Project to Increase Telegraph and Claremont Housing (PITCH) focuses on the
under-utilized Telegraph Avenue corridor, which offers significant capacity (estimated up to
5,700 new units), excellent transit access, and larger, more easily developable parcels.

By simultaneously applying targeted upzoning to auto-oriented sites in the highest-resource
Claremont neighborhood, the project would fulfill Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing (AFFH)
and other Housing Element goals by expanding housing opportunities into the highest-resource
parts of Berkeley. This strategic approach ensures a significant and geographically equitable
                                                                                           Page 51
                                          Page 10 of 12




contribution to Berkeley's housing goals, while potentially relieving pressure on more
commercially and architecturally sensitive areas.
FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS

The estimated cost to execute the necessary initial planning, staff time, any General Plan
amendments, and comprehensive environmental review (CEQA) for PITCH is $250,000. To
ensure the viability and timely commencement of this project, Councilmember Humbert commits
to funding:

   ●   $100,000180,000 from his Fiscal Year (FY) 2026 office budget.
   ●   $50,00070,000 from his FY 2027 office budget.

The total contribution of $1250,000 helps make the project viable for immediate launch, allowing
theany unexpected balance or contingency to be secured from other general City fundsthe
City’s general fund and/or grant opportunities in FY 2027/2028.

CURRENT SITUATION AND ITS EFFECTS

Telegraph Avenue, from Parker Street south to the Oakland border, is currently zoned C-C,
which sets a base zoning for development of 3-story mixed-use or residential buildings. This
represents a severe under-utilization of a key corridor located in a High Resource zone (slated
to be upgraded to a Highest Resource area by 2026 TCAC maps) and which features wide
street sections (~66’ of traffic lanes) and large parcels ideal for higher-density housing.

Furthermore, new housing production has been disproportionately low in the highest-resource
neighborhoods of District 8, such as Claremont. Although certain portions of the Claremont area
have recently seen their housing capacity increase due to the City’s Middle Housing efforts, fire
risks and a limited number of underutilized non-residential parcels mean that, absent this
project, this area is unlikely to contribute appreciably to the creation of new homes in Berkeley.

BACKGROUND

The City of Berkeley has a State mandate and made local commitments to increase housing
production in high-resource, transit-rich areas. Telegraph Avenue's length, infrastructure, and
under-developed parcels present an excellent opportunity for high-density housing. The State of
California's new law (SB 79) will already require upzoning near Ashby BART (District 3) and the
adjacent Telegraph/Alcatraz area, making a comprehensive upzoning of the entire Telegraph
corridor both a logical and proactive step.

RATIONALE FOR RECOMMENDATION

This project is an effective approach to better meeting Berkeley’s housing and equity goals:




                                                                                            Page 52
                                          Page 11 of 12




   ●   Increasing Housing Capacity on Telegraph: Rezoning Telegraph to C-T (8-stories) is
       projected to create a zoned housing capacity of up to around 5,700 units—a vital
       contribution to Berkeley’s housing stock.
   ●   Equitable Development:
            ○ The Telegraph corridor is located in a High Resource area and the project will
               better satisfy Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing goals.
            ○ Including the Claremont area (targeting auto-oriented uses near Ashby and
               Domingo) creates new housing opportunities in one of Berkeley’s highest
               resource neighborhoods, diversifying housing options and providing walkable
               retail access to residents.
   ●   Infrastructure Alignment: The project aligns new development with the City's investment
       in the Telegraph Multimodal Corridors project, which includes bus lanes and a protected
       cycletrack to improve transit and cycling infrastructure.

ENVIRONMENTAL SUSTAINABILITY AND CLIMATE IMPACTS

The project promotes environmental sustainability and climate action by increasing housing
development along the high-frequency 6-Telegraph AC Transit bus route, which also features
good access to jobs, shopping, and other urban amenities. This location encourages car-free or
car-lite living and would serve to reduce the carbon footprint associated with auto use relative to
housing in other areas of Berkeley and especially outside Berkeley.

Encouraging infill development also has the effect of reducing development pressure on
agricultural and wildland areas, helping preserve them as natural and working lands.

Subsequent completion of any required General Plan amendments and/or CEQA review will
ensure all environmental impacts are fully assessed and, if necessary, mitigated before zoning
changes are enacted.

POSSIBLE FUTURE ACTION

This referral represents the first step in a longer process that would involve substantial
additional staff work, public engagement, subsequent Council input, environmental review, and
subsequent Council votes to approve any rezoning, General Plan amendments, and associated
CEQA documentation.

FISCAL IMPACTS OF POSSIBLE FUTURE ACTION

The project is made more fiscally viable by the immediate financial commitment from
Councilmember Humbert's office. Subsequent fiscal impacts include increased property tax
revenue from new housing development, increased sales tax revenue from more residents and
subsequent foot traffic, and increased transit ridership on the 6-line, which will help AC Transit
address its fiscal shortfall.
