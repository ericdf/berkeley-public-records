RECOMMENDATION
Adopt a Resolution authorizing the City Manager to submit a nonbinding letter of
commitment of matching funds for a seismic Hazard Mitigation Grant application for the
City of Berkeley Civic Center Project-Seismic Retrofit of MSB and VMB to the Federal

(“VMB”).

FISCAL IMPACTS OF RECOMMENDATION
The City of Berkeley has been deemed eligible to apply for a grant of up to $16.5 million
as part of FEMA’s Hazard Mitigation Grant Program to seismically retrofit two historic
Civic Center buildings, the Maudelle Shirek Building (MSB) and the Veterans Memorial
Building (VMB), to address critical structural deficiencies and reduce collapse risk
during major earthquakes.

FEMA requires applicants to provide a letter committing to expend matching funds
equating to 25% of the award amount over the four-year (48 month) grant duration, for a
total project amount of $21.9 million. The City match portion of $5.4 million includes in-
kind expenditures for project management. The grant application includes a request for
grant management expenses, which do not require a local match. The projected grant
administrative costs of ($136,080.00) are well below the allowable 5%.

If awarded, the HMGP grant would provide nearly $16.5 million to the City of Berkeley to
seismically retrofit two historic Civic Center buildings, the Maudelle Shirek Building (MSB)
and the Veterans Memorial Building (VMB). These retrofits will transform both seismically
deficient structures into resilient community assets capable of withstanding design-level

The building-specific seismic retrofits include the following elements:

      The MSB (2134 Martin Luther King Jr Way), constructed in 1909, will be upgraded
       to a Damage Control Plus (DC+) performance level through a 2,760 sq. ft.
       reinforced concrete rear addition with 12-inch core walls, an 18-inch mat slab, and
       60 micropiles to create a continuous lateral load path, along with Fiber Reinforced
       Polymer (FRP) wrapping of select beams and columns and reinforcement of the
       spire and facade.

      The VMB (1931 Center Street), built in 1928, will be upgraded to BPON+ (Building
       Performance Objective Equivalent to New Standards) by strengthening
       diaphragms with new plywood sheathing, improving wall-to-diaphragm anchorage,
       adding steel collectors and chords, wrapping deficient concrete columns with FRP,
       and stabilizing tall walls with 12-inch shotcrete and exterior buttresses.

CURRENT SITUATION AND ITS EFFECTS
The Berkeley Civic Center Project will seismically retrofit two historic and critical
buildings: the Maudelle Shirek Building (MSB, Old City Hall) and the Veterans Memorial
Building (VMB) located at the heart of Berkeley’s Civic Center. MSB, built in 1909,
predates seismic codes and last underwent major renovation in the 1970s, which
excluded seismic upgrades. VMB, built in 1928, similarly lacks seismic design and last
received rehabilitation in 1993 without structural strengthening. Both buildings lie less
than two miles from the Hayward Fault, and sustained impacts from the 1989 Loma
Prieta (M6.9) and 2014 South Napa (M6.0) earthquakes. Both buildings are seismically
deficient pre-code structures and do not meet the American Society of Civil Engineers
(ASCE) 41-17 Basic Performance Objectives. They pose life safety hazards and risk
partial or total collapse in a major earthquake.

The Hazard Mitigation Grant Program (HMGP) funds plans and projects that reduce the
effects of future natural disasters. HMGP grants are funding provided by the Federal
Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) to states. In California, these funds are
administered by the Cal OES (Office of Emergency Services) HMGP Unit. The HMGP
grant requires a 25% local match of the total award, and if granted would be significant
($16.5M). Awarded projects must be cost-effective, demonstrated by a benefit-cost
ratio (BCR) of 1.0 or greater. The City of Berkeley Civic Center Project – Seismic
Retrofit of MSB and VMB grant, will be submitted with a combined BCR over 2.0.

The California Office of Emergency Services anticipates disbursing approximately many
millions in Hazard Mitigation Grant Program (HMGP) funding (the full scope and amount
won’t be known with precision until approximately January 2026), to reduce or eliminate
risks from future disasters. In May 2025, Berkeley submitted a Notice of Interest (NOI)
for a project to seismically retrofit two historic Civic Center buildings, the Maudelle
Shirek Building (MSB) and the Veterans Memorial Building (VMB), to address critical
structural deficiencies and reduce collapse risk during major earthquakes and was
invited to develop a full application; with a submittal deadline of September 15, 2025.

If awarded, the grant proposes the following for the Civic Center buildings: MSB will be
upgraded to Damage Control Plus (DC+), allowing it to withstand strong earthquakes
with repairable damage in weeks to months. VMB will be upgraded to BPON+
(equivalent to new building standards plus), ensuring localized damage only, with low
injury risk and repair timelines of months to a year. These upgrades will significantly
reduce collapse risk, preserve historic assets, and ensure continuity of civic use after a
disaster.

The proposed project is in conformance with Berkeley’s FEMA-approved Local Hazard
Mitigation Plan, which seeks to reduce the potential for loss of life, injury, and economic
damage from earthquakes, and must help protect and enhance quality of life in
Berkeley, by lessening buildings’ earthquake vulnerabilities that may pose risks to life
and/or emergency operations.1 In addition, the project will also advance Berkeley’s
Strategic Plan goals of (1) creating a resilient, safe, connected, and prepared city and
(2) providing state-of-the-art, well-maintained infrastructure, amenities, and facilities.

If awarded funding, this project will be co-managed by the City Manager’s Office (of
which the Office of Economic Development, or OED, is part) and the Department of
Public Works (PW) who is charged with all municipal building facility and maintenance
for the City of Berkeley. Attached to this report is a sample California Office of
Emergency Services form entitled, “Cal OES 130 – Designation of Applicant’s Agent
Resolution” (Exhibit A). Full completion of the Cal OES130 form will come after a
notification of award (if any) but is included herein for reference.

BACKGROUND
Berkeley's Civic Center is a National Register-listed historic district in Downtown
Berkeley, centering on Martin Luther King Jr. Civic Center Park and featuring significant
historic buildings like the Maudelle Shirek Building (formerly City Hall, or often referred
to as “Old City Hall”) and the Veterans Memorial Building. The area serves as a vital
public space for community events, and government functions, encompassing civic
buildings, green spaces, and educational facilities. Planning for the Civic Center is
guided by two documents both shaped by extensive stakeholder engagement; the Civic

After the adoption of the Civic Center Design Concept in 2023, the project moved to a
third phase, one that focuses on the rehabilitation of the buildings and the attainment of

Civic Center were built in the first quarter of the 1900s and have not been updated to

financial support to complete the proposed seismic retrofit projects to enhance the
structural integrity of both the MSB and VMB to be able to withstand a seismic event
and mitigate against the hazard posed by an earthquake.

ENVIRONMENTAL SUSTAINABILITY AND CLIMATE IMPACTS
Both buildings will be less likely to suffer damage in the event of an earthquake, and as
a result will be less likely than non-retrofitted structures to create additional
environmental impacts from collapse. If the mitigation projects prevent the buildings
from collapse, that would reduce injuries and local contamination from building debris.

HMGP funded efforts must undergo Environmental and Historic Preservation or EHP
review. In the case of the Civic Center grant, EHP and Permitting will be conducted in
collaboration with the project management team and the procured design consultant
and are allocated for in the combined cost estimate for MSB and VMB during Phase 1
of the grant scope of work. As stated in the grant application, Phase 1 tasks include:
Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act (NHPA) consultation and
documentation, NEPA/CEQA review, State Historic Preservation Officer (or SHPO)
Coordination, EHP clearance, and any other required tasks associated with EHP
review. Council shall retain final discretion to approve, impose mitigation measures, or
disapprove the HGMP-funded projects in light of any environmental impacts disclosed
as a result of the CEQA/NEPA review or any other reviews conducted as part of this
process.

RATIONALE FOR RECOMMENDATION
If the City receives funding under FEMA’s Seismic Hazard Mitigation Grant Program,
two remarkable and vulnerable structures in Berkeley’s Civic Center will be retrofitted,
preserving their historic fabric and ensuring continuity of essential civic and community
functions in the event of a major seismic event.

                                       Cal OES Form 130 Instructions
     A Designation of Applicant’s Agent Resolution for Non-State Agencies is required of all
     Applicants to be eligible to receive funding. A new resolution must be submitted if a
     previously submitted resolution is older than three (3) years from the last date of
     approval, is invalid, or has not been submitted.

     When completing the Cal OES Form 130, Applicants should fill in the blanks on pages 1
     and 2. The blanks are to be filled in as follows:

     Resolution Section:

     Governing Body: This is the group responsible for appointing and approving the
     Authorized Agents.
         Examples include: Board of Directors, City Council, Board of Supervisors, Board of
         Education, etc.

     Name of Applicant: The public entity established under the laws of the
     State of California.
          Examples include: School District, Office of Education, City, County or Non-profit
          agency that has applied for the grant, such as: City of San Diego, Sacramento
          County, Burbank Unified School District, Napa County Office of Education,
          University Southern California.

     Authorized Agent: These are the individuals that are authorized by the Governing Body
     to engage with the Federal Emergency Management Agency and the California
     Governor’s Office of Emergency Services regarding grants for which they have applied.
     There are two ways of completing this section:

           1. Titles Only: The titles of the Authorized Agents should be entered here, not their
              names. This allows the document to remain valid if an Authorized Agent leaves
              the position and is replaced by another individual. If “Titles Only” is the chosen
              method, this document must be accompanied by either a cover letter naming
              the Authorized Agents by name and title, or the Cal OES AA Names
              document. The supporting document can be completed by any authorized
              person within the Agency (e.g., administrative assistant, the Authorized Agent,
              secretary to the Director). It does not require the Governing Body’s signature.

           2. Names and Titles: If the Governing Body so chooses, the names and titles of the
              Authorized Agents would be listed. A new Cal OES Form 130 will be required if
              any of the Authorized Agents are replaced, leave the position listed on the
              document, or their title changes.

      Checking Universal or Disaster-Specific Box: A Universal resolution is effective for all
      past disasters and for those declared up to three (3) years following the date of
      approval. Upon expiration it is no longer effective for new disasters, but it remains
      in effect for disasters declared prior to expiration. It remains effective until the
      disaster goes through closeout unless it is superseded by a newer resolution.


     Governing Body Representative: These are the names and titles of the approving
     Board Members.

               Examples include: Chairman of the Board, Director, Superintendent, etc. The
               names and titles cannot be one of the designated Authorized Agents. A
               minimum of three (3) approving board members must be listed. If less than three
               are present, meeting minutes must be attached in order to verify a quorum was
               met.

     Certification Section:

     Name and Title: This is the individual in attendance who recorded the creation and
     approval of this resolution.

               Examples include: City Clerk, Secretary to the Board of Directors, County Clerk,
               etc. This person cannot be one of the designated Authorized Agents or
               Approving Board Member. If a person holds two positions (such as City Manager
               and Secretary to the Board) and the City Manager is to be listed as an
               Authorized Agent, then that person could sign the document as Secretary to
               the Board (not City Manager) to eliminate “Self-Certification.”
