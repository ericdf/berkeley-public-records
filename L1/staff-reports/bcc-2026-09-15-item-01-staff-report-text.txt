SUMMARY
This report requests City Council authorization for the City Manager or their designee to
submit a planning grant application up to the maximum eligible amount under the
California Strategic Growth Council (SGC) Community Resilience Centers (CRC)
Round 2 program for the Berkeley Civic Center. If awarded, this action authorizes the
City Manager to accept resulting grant funds and execute all related agreements. This
grant program does not require a local cash match of funds.

The Berkeley Civic Center, comprising the historic Martin Luther King Jr. Civic Center
Park, and the Maudelle Shirek (Old City Hall) and Veterans Memorial buildings,
currently faces seismic vulnerabilities and deferred maintenance. Securing CRC funding
will allow the City to advance necessary pre-development, planning, design,
environmental, architectural, engineering, and structural feasibility assessments
required to transform these historic spaces into a modernized network of resilient public
buildings for use by the community.

This grant application directly builds upon the City’s foundational investments in the
Civic Center Vision and Implementation Plan (approved September 2020) and the Civic
Center Design Concept (approved July 2023) previously funded by the Measure T1

FISCAL IMPACTS OF RECOMMENDATION
The anticipated amount of the Planning Grant from the CRC award could be up to
$500,000. This grant requires no matching funds from the City of Berkeley for the two-
year grant duration. If awarded, the City would then be eligible to apply for other
implementation grant funding that could underwrite a portion of the construction costs to
seismically retrofit two historic Civic Center buildings, the Maudelle Shirek Building or
“Old City Hall” and the Veterans Memorial Building. These retrofits will transform both
seismically deficient structures into resilient community assets capable of withstanding
design-level earthquakes with only limited, repairable damage, preserving their historic
fabric, establishing a community resilience center in the heart of Berkeley, and ensuring
continuity of essential civic and community functions in Berkeley’s Civic Center.

CURRENT SITUATION AND ITS EFFECTS
The Berkeley Civic Center represents an important, highly visible, yet severely
underutilized municipal footprint that currently fails to meet local resilience demands.
Both the historic “Old City Hall” or Maudelle Shirek Building (MSB) and the Veterans
Memorial Building (VMB) do not comply with modern structural safety regulations.
Because of the structural degradation and deferred maintenance, significant portions of
these properties are locked, vacant, or highly constrained, depriving the city of
dedicated community assembly spaces and forcing municipal bodies to pay annual
leasing fees to host public meetings elsewhere in Berkeley. Concurrently, the regional
landscape is experiencing an increase in climate-induced hazards, including hazardous
air quality from seasonal wildfires and extreme heat events, which disproportionately
threaten vulnerable urban populations lacking stable environmental refuge.

The Civic Center Vision and Implementation Plan (approved September 2020) and the
Civic Center Design Concept (approved July 2023) call to restore these two historic
buildings located at the heart of Berkeley’s Civic Center. MSB, built in 1909, predates
seismic codes and last underwent major renovation in the 1970s, which excluded
seismic upgrades and other modern resilient building features including energy
provision (i.e. electric and solar), and heating, venting, air conditioning or HVAC
systems. VMB, built in 1928, similarly lacks seismic design and last received
rehabilitation in 1993 without structural strengthening. Both buildings lie less than two
miles from the Hayward Fault. Both buildings pose life safety hazards and risk partial or
total collapse in a major earthquake and keep the buildings from serving Berkeley’s
public on an everyday (non-emergency) basis. Failing to secure state funding or

engineering, and site assessment work including further structural feasibility studies,
and environmental and site appraisals including funding for Phase I/II environmental site

permitting and pre-design activities including covering the cost of local permitting
coordination, regulatory zoning review, and initial environmental review and compliance.
Fiscal and policy analysis and operations planning are also allowable activities under
the CRC Planning Grant, though planning grant funding cannot be used to fund direct
construction, building renovations, or land acquisitions.

Grant Award Timeline
CRC Planning Grant applications are due in September 2026, with application and
review taking place through Winter 2027. After award announcements, selected lead
applicants enter a mandatory two-to-three-month post-award consultation process with
SGC staff to finalize the formal work plan, budget sheets, and tracking metrics before
signing the final grant agreement. The official grant performance period will begin in
Spring 2027 and conclude in 2029, establishing a two-year grant term. If awarded
funding, this project will be co-managed by the City Manager’s Office (of which the
Office of Economic Development, or OED, is part) and the Department of Public Works
(PW) which is charged with construction and maintenance of all municipal building
facilities for the City of Berkeley.

BACKGROUND
Berkeley's Civic Center is a National Register-listed historic district in Downtown
Berkeley, centering on Martin Luther King Jr. Civic Center Park and featuring significant
historic buildings including the Maudelle Shirek Building (formerly City Hall, or often
referred to as “Old City Hall”) and the Veterans Memorial Building. The area serves as a
vital public space for community events, and government functions, encompassing civic
buildings, green spaces, and educational facilities. Planning for the Civic Center is
guided by two documents both shaped by extensive stakeholder engagement; the Civic
Center Vision and Implementation Plan (adopted September 2020) and the Civic Center
Design Concept (adopted July 2023).1

After the adoption of the Civic Center Design Concept in 2023, the project moved to a
third phase, one that focuses on the rehabilitation of the buildings and the attainment of
funding for advancing the Civic Center Vision Plan and Design Concept. The two
structures, Veterans Memorial Building and the Maudelle Shirek Building, in the Civic
Center were built in the first quarter of the 1900s and have not been updated to current
seismic standards. The California Strategic Growth Council’s Community Resilience
Center (CRC) Planning grant would provide the City with necessary financial support to
advance the project to a “shovel ready” state. Once rehabilitated, the buildings would
become community resilience centers, providing refuge during severe climate hazards,
such as extreme heat and wildfire smoke, while delivering year-round community
programming.

ENVIRONMENTAL SUSTAINABILITY AND CLIMATE IMPACTS
Securing CRC Planning Grant funds directly advances the goals of Berkeley’s Climate
Action Plan (2009) and the Berkeley Resilience Strategy (2016), and related building
electrification frameworks, safety & disaster preparedness, and environmental justice
General Plan element updates (2025 & 2026) by improving energy and building
resilience and expanding localized emergency response capabilities with the
development of a community resilience center.

RATIONALE FOR RECOMMENDATION
Applying for CRC Planning Grant funding allows the City of Berkeley to leverage state
funds to advance the Civic Center Vision and Design Concept to a “shovel ready” state.
Authorizing this grant application also positions Berkeley’s Civic Center for future multi-
million-dollar allocations in subsequent implementation grant cycles to execute physical
construction, structural seismic retrofits, and microgrid infrastructure deployment.
