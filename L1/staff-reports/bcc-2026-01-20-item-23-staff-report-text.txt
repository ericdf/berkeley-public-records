Conduct a public hearing and upon conclusion, adopt a Resolution approving new fees and

FISCAL IMPACTS OF RECOMMENDATION
The proposed new fees and fee increases will provide additional revenue to offset the
operation and maintenance costs associated with Facility Rentals, Nature Center & Adventure
Playground Programs, Echo Lake, Berkeley Tuolumne and Berkeley Day Camp Programs,
and Youth Hoops League. Funding for recreation programs and facilities comes from the
General Fund, Camps Fund, and Marina Fund. Proposed fee increase revenue will be
deposited into the appropriate fund as described below.

These fee increases would raise an estimated $361,856 in FY 2027 to help offset program and
facility maintenance costs (see Table 1). The revenue assumptions below are based on
resident fees; non-resident fees are generally 20% higher, except, for example, at the Berkeley
Waterfront, where there is no residency requirement.

These fee adjustments will factor into budget reduction plan discussions. The Marina Fund
fees were discussed at the October 28, 2025 Council Budget & Finance Policy Committee
meeting1, as part of broader efforts to raise $100k in new Waterfront recreation program
revenue. (Note that the table below only includes revenue from new or increased fees; it does
not include revenue from expansion of afterschool programs and camps to the Waterfront.)
Camps Fund fees are needed to offset a projected $300k operating deficit. The balance will be
raised via additional changes in programming. General Fund fee revenue will be applied
toward Parks, Recreation & Waterfront Department budget reduction plans. If not adopted, the
department will need to generate alternate measures that will result in reductions to programs
and services.

CURRENT SITUATION AND ITS EFFECTS
On June 17, 2025, Council adopted an updated fee schedule for the Recreation Division
programs (Resolution No. 71,833-N.S.) The City regularly reviews all recreation fees in an
effort to keep programs and facilities accessible to residents while accounting for increased
costs to deliver those services. Proposed fee changes described below are proposed to go
into effect January 21, 2026.

Adventure Playground Birthday Party Package
Over the past years, City staff have received community feedback requesting expanded
access to birthday party packages that align with existing field trip experiences. Families have
expressed a desire for convenient, on-site celebrations that allow them to enjoy Adventure
Playground and programmed activities while also securing nearby amenities such as picnic
spaces and tables. While many residents currently reserve group rates for Adventure
Playground and then relocate outside facility boundaries for picnic use, the proposed birthday
party package consolidates all activity needs in one area, ensuring equitable access for all
users and improving operational efficiency.

The proposed birthday party package with a fee of $325 will offer families a 2-hour reservation,
a group orientation and their own covered space with picnic tables. Additional add-on is
available for $150 and will include 1-hour of structured activity facilitated by trained recreation
staff, access to designated private space, and curated equipment options depending on the
activity type. Revenue from the Adventure Playground Birthday Party Package will be
deposited in the Marina Fund.

Camps Fund Programs
PRW operates self-sustaining camp and recreation programs through the Camps Fund, a self-
supporting fund, with revenues expected to cover expenditures. The Camps Fund (CF) is
comprised of five programs:

        Day Camp, a summer day camp program in Berkeley;
        Cazadero Camp, operated by Cazadero Performing Arts Camp and offering music and
         performing arts programs, located in Sonoma County;
        Echo Lake Camp, offering Family Camp, Youth Camp, Counselor-in-Training and other
         programs, located near South Lake Tahoe; and
        Tuolumne Camp (BTC), offering Family Camp, Counselor-in-Training, and other
         programs, located near Yosemite National Park.
        Fee Classes, including internal and contractor-run classes in health and fitness, martial
         arts, youth enrichment, sports, and education.

Prior to the 2013 Rim Fire, which caused the destruction of BTC, the Camps Fund revenue
consistently outpaced expenses. Since that time the fund has operated in a deficit. While the
CF has made significant strides since BTC reopened four years ago, the operational deficit of
approximately $300,000, which is reduced from previous years, still exists. The proposed fee
increases will raise an estimated $245,909 in revenue in FY26 to help offset these costs.
Additional changes in programming at Echo Lake Camp and BTC are anticipated to offset
more of this deficit.

To cover rising costs in staffing, utilities, food, equipment and supplies, and transportation-
related expenses, fee increases are proposed of 10% in 2026 and 5% in 2027 for Echo Lake
Youth Camp and Counselor-in-Training (CIT), Berkeley Tuolumne Camp CIT, and Berkeley
Day Camp Core, AM, PM and CIT Programs (see table below). Attachment 2, Tables 9-12
compare proposed fees to those of similar programs in the region.

Berkeley Day Camp
Increased fees are required to offset costs of staffing, supplies and materials, field trip
expenses, and equipment related to operations. The proposed fee increase will help cover
costs, while maintaining program fees below market rate pricing compared to similar style
camps that are operating out of Berkeley and surrounding cities.

Berkeley Echo Lake Camp
Echo Lake Camp is embarking on its 102nd season of providing high quality, inclusive,
recreation opportunities for the Berkeley community. Echo Lake programming has grown
consistently since 2022 and fills all sessions with waitlists throughout the summer. Both
personnel and non-personnel costs have increased in recent years, and fee increases are
needed to offset the costs for youth camp and the counselor-in-training (CIT) programs. In
addition, fees are proposed for a new adult backpacking program at Echo Lake. This will be an
extension of our current Youth Backpacking program and will allow us to build a foundation for
Fall and Spring programming.

Berkeley Tuolumne Camp
The proposed increased fees for Berkeley Tuolumne Camp are needed to offset increasing
personnel and non-personnel costs associated with the CIT program. With these changes, our
program continues to be lower cost than similar programs in the region.

Fee Classes
The Recreation division offers various fee class programs, in partnership with contractors, who
offer specialized classes for the Community. Revenue from these classes is shared with the
contractor in various percentages based on the offering and the overall costs to the City.

Youth Hoops League
The City of Berkeley Youth Hoops Sports Program has had a longstanding history of offering
fun, competitive programs and leagues for the Berkeley youth community grades K-5th. Since
2017, we have sold out each program with extensive waitlists. Each season, we offer
participants over 12 practices once per week and 8 regular season games, with the chance to
make playoffs. Each practice is led by volunteer coaches, with city staff present to provide
support. Games are officiated by our sports official staff along with city staff who work our
book and scoreboard to ensure that teams are providing equal playing time to all players. All
equipment and gym space is provided by the City to teams for both practices and games. Prior
to the season we run observation nights to help ensure that teams are as balanced as possible
and we also run mandatory trainings for all our volunteer coaches. All players receive a
reversible jersey that they get to keep, as well as a small goodie bag at the end of the season.

Since 2022, there has been a 20% increase in supply costs as well as a 12% increase in
personnel costs. Increased fees are required to offset the growing cost of the programs,

YouthWorks Accelerator Program
The City is launching a new program within YouthWorks to place interns in professional
businesses. The Accelerator program will provide a limited number of local businesses with the
opportunity to have a summer intern (for a seven week period over the summer), who is a
trained City employee, who is new or relatively new to employment and who is versatile,
capable and ready to learn and support. The Accelerator interns get placed in a professional
level internship where they can receive a pre-career professional experience. The proposed
fee for professional businesses is up to $2,500, depending on the number of hours that their
intern works, to offset the City’s cost of employing the intern.

This will be in addition to the existing YouthWorks internship program, which provides
employment opportunities and life skills training that help participants transition into the
workforce. The YouthWorks program focuses on historically underserved communities and
aligns with the City of Berkeley’s commitment to racial equity, recognizing systemic barriers
and working to expand access to education, employment and self-sufficiency.

Scholarship Program
In 2025, the City provided over $300k in scholarships to Berkeley residents. The City offers
50% and 100% scholarships for a variety of programs in both the General Fund and the
Camps Fund, including Afterschool Programs, Swim Lessons, Summer Camps, Echo Lake
Youth Camp and BTC Family Camp.

City of Berkeley residents can apply annually to qualify for a scholarship. Scholarship
percentages are determined using HUD income guidelines. To qualify for a 100% scholarship,

BACKGROUND
The Parks, Recreation and Waterfront Department’s annual operational budget (excluding
capital improvement projects) is approximately $42 million. It is primarily supported by four
funds: the Parks Tax Fund, the Marina Fund, the Camps Fund, and the General Fund. The
Recreation Division relies predominantly on the General Fund and the Camps Fund to support
its programs. Program fees offset the costs associated with program delivery, facility
operations, and ongoing maintenance. Fees for programs and reservations tied to the self-
supporting Camps Fund, Parks Tax Fund, and Marina Fund are used to cover the costs of
providing these services.

The proposed fees were brought before the Parks, Recreation & Waterfront Commission on
November 12, 2025 at their regular meeting, and no action was taken. The Nature Center and
Adventure Playground fees were included as part of a broader set of fee increases to address
the projected Marina Fund deficit in a presentation to the Council Budget & Finance Policy
Committee on October 28, 2025.2

ENVIRONMENTAL SUSTAINABILITY AND CLIMATE IMPACTS
The proposed fee increases will support sustainability in these recreation programs which
provide direct and indirect environmental benefits. Recreation programs connect the
community with the environment, emphasize the importance of environmental stewardship,
and teach youth and community members about our local environmental resources and how to
preserve them.

I.    Scholarships/Low-Income Fee Support

      Eligibility
      Scholarships are available to Berkeley Residents who meet Federal low-income
      guidelines. The City of Berkeley offers partial (50%) or full (100%) scholarships to
      Berkeley Residents for a wide variety of Recreation Division Programs. Scholarship
      eligibility is based upon Housing and Urban Development (HUD) income thresholds,
      and applicants must submit documents to verify their proof of residency and gross
      household income or submit a low-income affidavit form to qualify.

             100% Scholarship: Applicants must meet HUD’s current “Very Low Income
              Limits.”
             50% Scholarship: Applicants must meet HUD’s current “Very Low Income
              Limits” plus the difference between the “Low Income Limits” and the “Very
              Low Income Limits.”

      The most up-to-date version of HUD’s Income Limits can be found here:
      https://www.huduser.gov/portal/datasets/il.html

      Annual Renewal Process
      Following initial approval for a scholarship, applicants must renew their scholarship
      eligibility annually by submitting current proof of Berkeley residency and income.
      City of Berkeley Recreation Administration Staff will reach out to scholarship
      recipients annually to request renewal documentation.

II.   Special Use Rental Policy

      1. A primary goal of the City's Community Centers is to become a "hub" of
         resources and referrals for neighborhoods by establishing a relationship of
         mutual support and trust. Through this trust, we promote safe neighborhoods and
         opportunities for families and youth to be engaged in activities that enhance the
         community and support the goals of the Parks Recreation and Waterfront
         Department. Each year the Parks Recreation and Waterfront Department
         receives numerous requests from community groups to meet at its Community
         Centers as well as the Berkeley Marina Conference Room for no fee. As a way to
         cultivate community relationships and respond to community requests for free
         meeting space, the City has created a policy whereby qualified local groups (e.g.,
         neighborhood, Community Agency Contract, or non-profit groups that comply
         with this policy) are allowed to hold meetings, free of charge, at the City's

2. Application Process
   A completed application and attachments must be submitted before a reservation
   is confirmed. Groups applying for a Special Use Rental must support and benefit
   the goals of the City of Berkeley Parks Recreation and Waterfront Department
   ("PRW"). The department is committed to administering excellent recreation
   programs at our community centers, camps, marina and parks; developing new
   programs that improve access for 18 Exhibit В historically underserved
   participants; maintaining our trees and municipal urban forest, landscaping and
   infrastructure; and, developing Capital Projects with community input to improve
   and enhance residents' park and facility experience.

   All interested groups must submit a completed Special Use Rental application on
   an annual basis, with all of the following attached:

         Proof that membership has approved meetings (either by providing a
          Board resolution, Agendas, or Minutes).
         A $200 Cleaning/Damage Deposit (Refundable).
         A written statement that the group/non-profit organization does not
          discriminate on the basis of race, color, religion, ancestry, national origin,
          age, sex, sexual orientation, marital status, political affiliation, veterans'
          status, disabilities (physical and mental), and medical condition (including
          cancer and HIV status).
         A written statement describing the group's goals and how they support
          and benefit the goals of the City of Berkeley Parks Recreation and
          Waterfront Department.

   In addition to the above requirements that apply to all interested groups,
   Community Agency Contract and non-profit groups must also submit:
        List of membership roster with names, addresses, and phone numbers
        Proof of non-profit status (required for non-profit groups)
        At least 14 days before the date of the first meeting, qualified groups must
          provide a valid Certificate of Insurance (COI) containing the City's
          minimum liability requirements, and naming the city of Berkeley as
          additional insured on separate Endorsement. This COI must cover all of
          the group's reserved dates for the year. If the COI is not provided at least
          14 days prior to the first reserved date, the reservation will be cancelled.

   PRW will not begin to review the application until the application is complete,
   including all required information and attachments. A reservation is not confirmed
   until the applicant receives written notice of approval from PRW.

   Division Office, 1720 8th St., Berkeley, CA 94710; 3) Berkeley Marina Office; or

   Approval of applications is at the discretion of the Recreation and Youth Services
   Manager or designee; a decision denying the application is final and not subject
   to appeal. If PRW denies the fee waiver application, the interested group may
   apply to rent the room for the standard fee.

   Qualified Groups: In order to qualify for this policy, groups must comply with all of
   the requirements set forth above in section I. In addition, groups must support

         Neighborhood Groups: Any group living in a specific area of Berkeley that

         Non-profit Groups: Any organized non-profit group that meets at least
          quarterly.
         Community Agency Contract Groups: Any City of Berkeley Community
          Agency Contract group that serves youth under 18 years of age.

   There must be adult leaders, advisors and/or chaperones attending each

       Meetings must be open to the public.
       Regular Business Operating Hours are when the Community Center or
         Marina Office is open (Hours may vary for each facility based on its
