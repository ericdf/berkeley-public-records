INTRODUCTION
On January 24, 2025, the City Auditor released, 2009 Lease Audit Follow-Up – Berkeley
Faces the Same Risks 16 Years Later1 to City Council with recommendations to
improve the City’s lease oversight and management. The first status report was
submitted to Council on October 28, 2025. This information item provides Council with
an update on the implementation status of the report’s recommendations. This is the
second status report regarding this audit.

CURRENT SITUATION AND ITS EFFECTS
The audit included eight recommendations. As of October 2026, staff have started
implementation for six recommendations, one is partially implemented, and one has not
started because it requires completion of the other seven before it can commence.
While work on seven recommendations is ongoing, the only change of status since the
last status report on October 28, 2025 is associated with recommendation 8.2.2

Please see Attachment 1 for a detailed table of audit report recommendations,
corrective action plans, and implementation progress. The next status report to Council
is expected in Fall 2027.

The partially implemented recommendation, 8.2, states, “Public Works and Finance
should determine if the existing systems can be used to finalize a comprehensive list of
City leases.” The Auditor’s finding provided further direction to assess existing, planned,
and other software systems to, “enable more effective oversight of leases.” Public
Works and Finance concluded the current system is insufficient for the City’s needs. A
new software system capable of including information for all City owned properties and
the ability to manage leases, license agreements, insurance documents, and creating

Staff conducted a thorough review of available options and determined that a cloud-

processing is in progress.

All recommendations require the formation of an interdepartmental team of staff
involved in lease and license agreement tracking and management. A Lease
Management Ad Hoc Team was formed and completed the following since the last
update:
   1. Built an interdepartmental shared lease list and presented it to the City Budget
      and Finance Committee on April 9, 2026. Please see Attachment 3 for copies of
      the list and presentation.
   2. Documented inconsistencies in data collected by different departments and used
      the information to create the software needs checklist.
   3. Recommended an affordable and proven lease and property management
      software provider for City Management consideration. Software has been
      selected and implementation will begin no later than December 2026.
BACKGROUND
On February 11, 2025, the City Auditor presented 2009 Leases Audit Follow-Up -
Berkeley Faces the Same Risks 16 Years Later, to City Council. The new report
assessed the implementation of recommendations from the 2009 audit and concluded
that the City still faces the same issues and lacks a clear approach to lease
management, a relevant policy to guide leasing and licensing decisions, and a complete
central inventory of leases and license agreements. The report asserts that these
factors continue to compromise the City’s ability to strategically manage City-owned
property and reopened eight prior recommendations that require collaboration across
departments to better ensure sound management of City-owned and occupied
properties.

Public Works and the City Manager’s Office accepted the Auditor’s findings
and guidance for implementation. The recommendations were re-opened and all parties
agreed to provide annual updates on implementation until fully completed.

CITY OF BERKELEY
REAL PROPERTY SOFTWARE NEEDS


The City of Berkeley needs software that simpliﬁes managing a small but complex lease
portfolio by centralizing data, automating ﬁnancial compliance, and providing proactive
management tools. The software should act as a "single source of truth," allowing teams in
Finance, PW real estate, PRW, HHCs and CAO to collaborate using the same up-to-date
information.

Training and Expansion

While the software will initially be used by Public Works to meet its requirement to maintain
citywide property and lease lists, assurance is needed that, if desired, expanding use and
access to other departments is possible without increasing the cost to the City.

    Include unlimited users and the ability to control each user’s access to di erent
     aspects of the system.

    Include comprehensive training for current and future users; trainers should be
     experienced with the software but also have lease expertise and accounting expertise.

    Include the ability to track and manage all City owned properties, not just those
     occupied by third-party tenants.

Key Capabilities Needed

      Centralized Data Repository: To consolidate all lease documents, contracts, and utility
       bills into one platform, breaking down data silos between departments.

      Automated Accounting & Compliance: To automate calculations GASB 87 and other
       required reporting to stated standards, generate journal entries, and produce
       disclosure reports to ensure audit readiness.

      Proactive Alerts & Date Management: Ability to set custom notiﬁcations for critical
       dates such as renewals, expirations, rent increases, and insurance deadlines to avoid
       penalties or missed opportunities.

      AI-Powered Data Extraction (not a must but a nice to have): AI automated extraction of
       key data points from complex lease agreements, reducing manual entry errors.

      Seamless Integrations: It connects with major ERP and ﬁnancial systems (like ERMA)
       to sync lease information with broader business processes. Flat ﬁles are preferred to
       start to ensure seamless future integration without compromising ERMA.

      Sustainability & ESG Reporting: A platform that can track environmental data—such as
       energy, waste, and water usage—to help the City have metrics for environmental,
       social, and governance reporting.

The following checklist aligns with typical municipal real property duties, such as managing
lease agreements, tracking critical dates, and ensuring ﬁnancial transparency.

Essential Management Tasks

      Centralize Lease Inventory: Locate and consolidate all city leases from various
       departments into a single "source of truth".

      Monitor Critical Dates: Set automated alerts for lease renewals, expirations, rent
       increases, and insurance deadlines to prevent penalties.

      Manage Clause & Obligation Tracking: Track speciﬁc city requirements, such as ADA
       compliance, maintenance responsibilities, and use restrictions directly within lease
       records.

      Automate Appropriation-Based Options: Use system workﬂows to manage annual
       rollovers for leases tied to speciﬁc funding cycles.

Standard & Custom Reporting

      GASB 87 & GASB 96 Compliance Reports: Generate audit-ready disclosure reports,
       including roll-forward reports and amortization schedules for both real estate and
       software subscriptions (GASB 96 Subscription-Based Information Technology
       Arrangements (SBITAs), requires government entities to recognize a right-to-use
       intangible asset and a corresponding subscription liability for cloud computing and
       software contracts, similar to lease accounting under GASB 87. It applies to contracts
       over 12 months, e ective for ﬁscal years starting after June 15, 2022.).

      Sustainability & ESG Reports: Track energy and emissions data to align with municipal
       climate action plans and meet potential net-zero reporting goals.

      Portfolio Health Dashboards: Visualize lease locations and costs through interactive
       maps and high-level summaries for presentation to the City Council.

      Budget & Forecasting Models: Model lease cost impacts within constrained
       appropriation cycles to assist in annual city budget planning.

System Integrations

      Financial & ERP Systems: Integrate with city ﬁnancial software (like ERMA) to sync
       journal entries and lease payments with the broader general ledger.

      Accounts Receivable: Streamline the collection of rent from third-party tenants leasing
       city-owned property.

      EPA Portfolio Manager: Export facility data directly to the EPA's benchmarking tool to
       simplify public-sector transparency requirements.

      GIS Integration: Connect lease records with the city’s GIS (Geographic Information
       System) for spatial analysis of city-owned and leased land parcels.



Communication Needs

It is more important that real property software act as an internal collaboration and data
centralization resource rather than acting as a direct two-way messaging app for tenants. City
of Berkeley needs a system of record for ﬁnance, legal, and real estate teams across multiple
departments. It is more important that the software can be set up to send standard and
programmable alerts and reminders to the tenants AND to the assigned manager of the lease
in each department to ensure they communicate with the tenant by phone or email.

Software must support communication in the following ways:

    Internal Collaboration: Multiple departments (Finance, Real Estate, Legal) can access
     the same lease data and leave notes or track approvals within a controlled
     environment to ensure everyone is aligned.

    Contact Management: Store and track all contact details for every lease, including
     tenants, property managers, and attorneys, to quickly ﬁnd who you need to reach via
     traditional channels.

    External Integration for Alerts: The platform can integrate with communication tools
     like Outlook. This allows the system to push critical alerts (like renewal deadlines or
     rent changes) to the appropriate team members who can then reach out to tenants.

    Operational Transparency: By tracking performance and building occupancy, the
     software provides data that helps the City in its role as property owners "improve

    Visual and GIS-Friendly: Highly visual and user-friendly, with interactive maps and a

    Lease Life Focus: must be designed to handle the entire life of the lease after
     compliance is met, such as tracking CAM (Common Area Maintenance)
     reconciliations and legal disputes.

    Fiscal Communication: Allows allocation of leases across di erent municipal funds
     and prepares modiﬁed accrual journal entries easily.

   Desire Operational Impact of the Software

    Cost Savings: Will identify overpayments and non-conforming charges through
     automated audits, the software helps control expenses and optimize space utilization.
     Speciﬁcally need to address late payment, nonpayment, and underpayment of rent.

    Enhanced Visibility: City leadership can use real-time dashboards and interactive
     maps to visualize the entire portfolio of properties and make data-driven decisions
     regarding acquisitions and/or sale of property and space planning.

    Risk Mitigation: Robust internal controls and a complete audit trail for all lease
     modiﬁcations help maintain data integrity and reduce the risk of non-compliance
     particularly with regard to third-party insurance requirements.
