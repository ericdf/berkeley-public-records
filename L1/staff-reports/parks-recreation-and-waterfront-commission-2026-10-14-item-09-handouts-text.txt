BACKGROUND
In November 2016, Berkeley voters approved Measure T1, a $100M Infrastructure Bond
Measure. The measure required 2/3 support to pass and received approval from 86.5% of
Berkeley voters. The ballot question read:

         Shall the City of Berkeley issue general obligation bonds not exceeding $100,000,000
         to repair, renovate, replace, or reconstruct the City’s aging infrastructure and facilities,
         including sidewalks, storm drains, parks, streets, senior and recreation centers, and
         other important City facilities and buildings?

Measure T1 was originally structured in three four-year phases for project selection, bond
sales, and implementation. The City ultimately consolidated project selection into two
phases, with community input and approval by the two Lead Commissions and City Council
in 2017 ($35 million) and 2020 ($45 million). Bonds were issued in three installments, with
the final $20 million sold in June 2026. All $100 million in bond funding is now fully
committed to existing projects. All projects are estimated to be completed by FY28.

Community Engagement
Projects that were funded by Measure T1 resulted from 80 public process meetings that
occurred in 2017 and in 2020. In addition to that, there were 100 public meetings focused
on specific projects. To keep the community informed about Measure T1, quarterly reports
are released to a mailing list and posted on the City’s website.

Management and Oversight
The City’s Bond Management Team meets bi-weekly and is comprised of leadership and
staff from the Public Works (PW) and Parks, Recreation & Waterfront (PRW) Departments.
This staff includes Directors, Deputy Directors, Manager of Engineering, Senior
Management Analysts, Fiscal Managers and Civil Engineers. The management team is
supported by a T1-funded Associate Management Analyst. A subset of the Management
Team overseas program budgeting, expenditures, and bond administration.

Five full-time equivalent (FTE) positions were allocated to Measure T1 through FY26.
Staffing levels have changed over time as projects get completed and workloads are
adjusted. Measure T1 is charged only for actual staff time worked on T1 projects. City
overhead costs (e.g., executive management, technology, human resources, and finance)
are not charged to Measure T1.

A Measure T1 Bond Policies and Procedures Manual was developed in 2017 which provides
guidance on all aspects of the administration of the program. Any changes in projects or the
phase of a project are reviewed by the Parks, Recreation & Waterfront Commission and
Transportation and Infrastructure before a recommendation is made to the City Council.
Audits by an independent auditor occur every two years to ensure that Measure T1 funds
are spent correctly.

T1 Audits
The Measure T1 bond program has been the subject of an independent audit every two
years since the program began, for a total of four completed audits (2017-2019; 2019-2021;
2021-2023; 2023-2025). The four independent audits have verified that Measure T1 funds
are being spent appropriately. The four independent audit reports can be found at the
following link on the City’s Measure T1 webpage: Measure T1: Infrastructure and Facilities
Bond | City of Berkeley (scroll to “Management and Oversight”).

into Measure T1 projects, as required by Resolution No. 67,795-N.S. Public art has been
included in many T1 projects, including North Berkeley Senior Center, Willard Club House,

CONCLUSION
Nearly ten years after Berkeley voters approved Measure T1, the City has made substantial
progress in addressing aging infrastructure and improving public facilities throughout the
community. By combining the $100 million in voter-authorized bond funding with grants,
State and Federal funding, and other resources, the City has assembled more than $183
million to support 76 infrastructure projects. These investments have improved streets,
sidewalks, parks, recreation facilities, public buildings, and critical infrastructure serving
Berkeley residents. The program has been guided by extensive community engagement,
City Council and commission oversight, regular public reporting, and independent financial
reviews.

With 66 of the 76 funded projects completed and the remaining projects advancing toward
an anticipated completion by FY 2028, Measure T1 is entering its final stage. Staff will
continue to oversee project delivery, manage available resources, provide public updates,
and address outstanding construction and funding challenges. While Measure T1 has
enabled significant improvements to Berkeley's public infrastructure, substantial capital
improvement and deferred maintenance needs remain beyond the scope of the original
bond authorization. The experience gained through Measure T1, including leveraging
outside funding, engaging the community, and adapting project scopes to changing costs
and conditions, provides a foundation for the City's ongoing management of its infrastructure
and capital improvement programs.

BACKGROUND
The City’s consideration of a 2026 General Obligation bond measure is grounded in a
series of City Council referrals, long-range infrastructure planning efforts, cross-
departmental analysis, voter survey research, and community engagement activities
focused on Berkeley’s aging public infrastructure and long-term capital needs.

Berkeley faces significant unfunded infrastructure and deferred maintenance needs
across public safety facilities, parks and recreation facilities, civic buildings, sidewalks,
ADA accessibility improvements, seismic upgrades, waterfront infrastructure, and other
critical public assets. Current estimates indicate that the City’s long-term infrastructure
needs exceed $1.5 billion.2 In addition, the proposed Capital Improvement Program for
Fiscal Years 2027-2031 identifies over $2.1 billion in needs.3 Existing capital funding
sources and annual appropriations are insufficient to address these needs at the scale
and pace required.

This work builds upon the City’s broader infrastructure planning framework, including
Vision 2050, which emphasizes climate resilience, equity, public health and safety,
sustainability, accessibility, and integrated long-term infrastructure planning. The
proposed bond framework is also informed by prior infrastructure planning efforts,
deferred maintenance analyses, facility assessments, and capital improvement planning
work conducted across multiple City departments.

potential bond projects and infrastructure priorities. Feedback consistently emphasized
interest in life safety, emergency response, accessibility, climate resilience, parks and
recreation facilities, sidewalks, public buildings, and other visible neighborhood
infrastructure improvements. Community members also emphasized the importance of
accountability, transparency, geographic equity, and implementation feasibility.

ENVIRONMENTAL SUSTAINABILITY AND CLIMATE IMPACTS
Many of the infrastructure investments under consideration for a potential November
2026 ballot measure directly advance the City’s environmental sustainability, climate
resilience, and environmental justice goals. Modernizing aging public facilities provides
opportunities to incorporate energy-efficient building systems, high-performance
materials, and low-carbon construction practices consistent with Berkeley’s Climate
Action Plan policies.

Several of the proposed project categories and example projects also directly support
climate adaptation and resilience objectives, including improvements to stormwater
infrastructure, shoreline and waterfront protection, seismic upgrades to critical facilities,
and retrofits that enhance emergency response capacity during climate-related
emergencies such as extreme heat, wildfire smoke events, flooding, and power
outages. Investments in public safety facilities and emergency communications
infrastructure further strengthen the City’s ability to respond to increasingly frequent and
severe climate-driven hazards.

In addition, proposed investments in sidewalks, ADA accessibility, and pedestrian
infrastructure advance sustainable transportation and public health outcomes by
improving walkability and accessibility, reducing reliance on vehicle travel, and
supporting safer, more connected neighborhoods. Enhancements to parks, recreation
facilities, and community centers also contribute to urban greening, heat mitigation, and
