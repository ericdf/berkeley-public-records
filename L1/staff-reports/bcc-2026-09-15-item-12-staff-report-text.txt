Subject:         Grant Application: Safe Routes to Transit and Bay Trail Program (SR2TBT)

RECOMMENDATION
Adopt a Resolution authorizing the City Manager or his designee to submit a grant
application to the Metropolitan Transportation Commission’s Regional Measure 3 Safe
Routes to Transit and Bay Trail Program (SR2TBT) for up to $25,000,000 for
construction of portions of the landside elements of the Berkeley Water Transportation
Pier-Ferry Project.

FISCAL IMPACTS OF RECOMMENDATION
If awarded, the Metropolitan Transportation Commission’s (MTC) Regional Measure 3
(RM3) Safe Routes to Transit and Bay Trail Program (SR2TBT) grant will provide up to
$25,000,000 for the construction of portions of the landside elements of the Berkeley
Water Transportation Pier-Ferry (BWTPF) Project (“Project”). The grant program does
not require matching funds.

CURRENT SITUATION AND ITS EFFECTS
The Berkeley Pier has been closed since 2015 due to observed structural defects. In
2018, WETA staff initiated discussions with City staff about the possibility of a new ferry
terminal at the Berkeley Waterfront. In March 2019, WETA and the City Council
authorized an MOU Agreement to jointly fund a planning study on the feasibility of
rebuilding the Berkeley Pier for ferry service and recreational access (Resolution No.
68,782, adopted on March 12, 2019). The findings of the Feasibility Study, including the
preferred alternative, were presented during a City Council Work Session on December
7, 2021.
The current design and environmental review phase is funded by three sources: a) the
project received $2,960,000 as part of a one-time allocation from the state of $15 million
for Waterfront improvement projects, administered by the State Coastal Conservancy
(Resolution No. 70,629, adopted on December 13, 2022); b) in May 2023, the project
received a $5,139,000 grant from Alameda County Transportation Commission (ACTC)
for the environmental analyses and permitting for the Project (Resolution No. 70,950,
adopted on July 11, 2023); and c) in May 2024, the City and WETA amended the MOU

The total Project involves the construction of in-water elements (a new public pier and
new ferry landing dock with electric vehicle charger), and landside elements (new plaza,
new Bay Trail segments, rebuilt sections of University Ave and Seawall Drive with a
Class IV cycle track, sidewalks, crossings, and bioretention, and a rebuilt Seawall
Parking Lot), along with numerous public amenities (picnic areas, restroom, benches,
drinking fountains, washdown area, etc). To-date, construction funding for the project
has not been identified. The current construction cost estimate for the entire Project is
$105.8M (based on the 60% level of design).
On June 5, 2018, Bay Area voters approved Regional Measure 3 (RM3), and on
December 19, 2018, the Bay Area Toll Authority (BATA) adopted a toll schedule to
phase in the resulting toll increases. BATA implemented the first and second dollars of
the toll increase on January 1, 2019, and January 1, 2022, respectively. The
Metropolitan Transportation Commission (MTC) is listed as the project sponsor and is
responsible for administering a competitive process for the Safe Routes to Transit &
Bay Trail (SR2TBT) program. The program aims to fund projects to increase the
carrying capacity and travel options on Bay Area bridges and along bridge corridors,
reduce greenhouse gas emissions, increase active transportation, reduce congestion,
and improve real and perceived safety. The Bay Trail project's mission is to complete
the vision of the 500-mile walking and bicycling shoreline trail that serves Bay Area
residents' recreation and active transportation needs. Applications for the current cycle
SR2TBT program funds (cycle 2) are due on October 5, 2026 and the City will submit a
grant proposal for up to $25M to construct portions of the landside elements of the
Project.
This resolution authorizes the preparation and submittal of a grant application for the
construction of portions of landside elements of the Project. The review of any potential
environmental impacts per the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) is required
prior to City approval of the Project or commencement of construction on the Project
improvements. Further, construction cannot begin until acquisition of all applicable local,
state and federal permits.

BACKGROUND
In July 2015, the Pier was closed to the public indefinitely due to structural safety
issues. On June 21, 2017, after conducting a competitive RFP process, the City
contracted with GHD, Inc. to perform a structural engineering assessment to identify
options and costs for repairing the Pier (Contract No. 10632, Resolution No. 67,856).
The final study identified 11 conceptual options ranging in cost from $22 million to $65
million in 2023 dollars. Since completion of that assessment in 2021, the Pier has
continued to structurally degrade. Structural engineers currently working on the Pier

Ferry Project have concluded that the Pier has reached the end of its useful life and

In 2023, the City initiated the design and environmental phase of the Project to
complete detailed design, environmental analyses and permitting for the preferred
concept. In summary, this phase of work includes:

       Detailed engineering analyses including coastal, geotechnical, parking,
        transportation demand management and sea level rise studies;
       Environmental studies, including existing conditions and project impact analyses;
       Development of an Environmental Impact Report (EIR) in compliance with
        California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA);
       Analyses and documentation for compliance with the National Environmental
        Protection Act (NEPA);
       Public outreach and engagement;
       Project permitting, including City, state and federal permitting agencies with
        jurisdiction over the Project; and
       Development of bid-ready Project plans and specifications.

The Project involves several state and federal agencies with jurisdiction over portions of
the Project. These include California State Lands Commission, the Bay Conservation
and Development Commission (BCDC), the United States Army Corps of Engineers
(USACE), National Marine Fisheries Service (NMFS), United States Fish and Wildlife
Service (USFWS), California Department of Fish and Wildlife (CDFW) and the Regional
Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB). City staff have initiated early consultation with
these agencies and will continue to work closely with them to coordinate permit
applications, reviews, compliance and issuance. Project construction would not begin
until applicable permits for the work were issued by all agencies with jurisdiction.

The Draft EIR was published in February 2026 for a 60-day public review period. The
Final EIR is anticipated to be considered by Council in Fall, 2026. The Project is
currently at 60% design development, with 90% design documents anticipated to be
completed in Fall, 2026.

ENVIRONMENTAL SUSTAINABILITY AND CLIMATE IMPACTS
The total project would install a new public pier for recreation and a new, zero-emission
electric public ferry service between Berkeley and San Francisco. The City’s Climate
Action Plan identifies public transit as a more sustainable form of transportation
(Chapter 3) and sets a goal to expand under-used modes of transportation, such as
ferry service at the Berkeley Marina to connect to San Francisco and other locations.
The Project also includes multiple active transportation / multi-modal zero-emission
improvements such as a new bike lane on University Avenue and installation of a
section of Bay Trail.

and on December 19, 2018, the Bay Area Toll Authority (BATA) adopted a toll schedule

WHEREAS, the SR2TBT program aims to fund projects to reduce greenhouse gas
emissions, increase active transportation, reduce congestion, and complete the vision of
the 500-mile walking and bicycling Bay Trail; and

WHEREAS, the Project must undergo environmental impact review per the California
Environmental Quality Act (CEQA), and the City cannot approve the Project or initiate
construction until the necessary review has been completed; and

WHEREAS, the Project must obtain permits from local, state and federal agencies with
jurisdiction over portions of the Project and the City cannot initiate construction until the
necessary permits have been issued.

NOW THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Council of the City of Berkeley that the
City Manager or his designee is authorized to submit a grant application to the
Metropolitan Transportation Commission’s Regional Measure 3 Safe Routes to Transit
and Bay Trail Program (SR2TBT) for up to $25,000,000 for construction of portions of
the landside elements of the Berkeley Water Transportation Pier-Ferry Project.
