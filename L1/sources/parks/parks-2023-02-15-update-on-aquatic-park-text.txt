Office of the City Manager




February 15, 2023


To:              Honorable Mayor and Members of the City Council
From:            Dee Williams-Ridley, City Manager
Re:              Update on Aquatic Park

We want to provide you with an update on Aquatic Park, where we have looked into the recent
deaths of some leopard sharks and bat rays at Aquatic Park, where, apparently, the historic
January rains changed the salinity in an unprecedented and deadly fashion for certain saltwater
creatures.

This artificial lagoon created as part of the construction of I-80 in the 1930s combines storm
water run-off along with water from the Bay, creating brackish water that has hosted a wide
variety of marine life. Thanks to the repair of the five tide tubes, completed in 2022 as one of our
T1 projects, we now see more fish and other creatures in the lagoon than before.

City staff from three departments – Parks, Recreation and Waterfront; Health Housing &
Community Services; and Public Works – as well as a contracted environmental consultant
have been looking into this issue since last week. We have also been working with the
California Department of Fish and Wildlife.

We have been told that the marine life that has died in the lagoon – leopard sharks and bat rays
– are more dependent on high salinity. The January rains were among the most severe in
history, and the experts and agencies that we have been consulting with have identified the
changed salinity from the storm runoff as the likely cause.

There are no water quality concerns at this time.

The repair of the tide tubes was timely. In the past, the failed tubes meant that we regularly saw
flooding during heavy rains. The tube restoration required coordination with five agencies and
extensive prep work, which was led by PRW. This year, during rains that some believe have not
been seen for a century, we did not see any flooding at the lagoon. We have also not seen
widgeon grass nor algae. We will have to continue to observe how this transformed habitat
evolves.

City Staff continues to work with state fish and wildlife to better understand the issue.
