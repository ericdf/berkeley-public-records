                                  EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The proposed Berkeley Innovation Zone project would redevelop a 1.86-acre Berkeley campus
site in downtown Berkeley to create a public-facing node for life sciences, materials science, and
climate research. The project would underscore UC Berkeley’s leading role in these fields of
research and innovation. The Berkeley Innovation Zone project would include new buildings – a
South Building and North Building totaling approximately 486,000 gross square feet – with
academic research and collaboration space, parking, and open space. The South Building project
would be a donor development project and would provide wet laboratory and flexible scale-up
space for campus faculty and researchers and campus-affiliated entrepreneurs and their
collaborators to incubate startup companies that provide solutions for energy and the
environment. The project site is currently occupied by University Hall, two Regents-owned
commercial properties, and surface parking. The existing buildings and infrastructure would be
demolished in a Site Preparation project that would be completed prior to construction of the two
new buildings and open space. The campus will return to the Regents to request approval of the
North Building project.

The Berkeley Innovation Zone will support diversity, equity, and inclusion by providing
inclusive interaction space for a diverse set of researchers, innovators, and entrepreneurs working
at the forefront of addressing critical challenges related to energy and the climate, and research
related to life sciences. The Berkeley Innovation Zone project would meet the UC Sustainable
Practices Policy targets, including being 100 percent electric. The project would address seismic,
restoration and renewal through the demolition of University Hall (Seismic Performance Rating
VI).

                                        BACKGROUND

UC Berkeley’s academic mission is to push the boundaries of knowledge, challenge convention,
and expand opportunity to create the leaders of tomorrow. This mission is advanced by making
discovery the foundation of the Berkeley experience; empowering students with the skills and
support needed to become pioneers of new knowledge and leaders in a broad range of fields;
addressing society’s greatest challenges through groundbreaking research; and embracing
diverse, inclusive, and entrepreneurial academic excellence.

The Berkeley Innovation Zone supports the mission by providing new space for interdisciplinary
research, discovery, and entrepreneurship with key project planning objectives:
    • Providing life-science research and wet laboratory space to support UC Berkeley’s
       academic mission, to expand its research enterprise, and to accelerate cutting-edge
       discovery and innovation in life sciences and climate research.
    • Locating UC Berkeley’s life sciences and climate research together in a manner that
       enables intellectual exchange, interdisciplinary discovery, interaction and collaboration
       between academic programs and disciplines, and encourages collaboration.

   •   Fostering a collaborative ecosystem that attracts top-tier researchers, innovators, and
       industry leaders, propelling the next generation of life sciences and climate technologies,
       fueling scientific progress, and creating a hub to support economic growth, positive
       societal impact, and the University’s public service values.
   •   Providing academic and research facilities in the City Environs adjacent to the Campus

       deficiencies in aging buildings by demolishing and replacing them with new state-of-the-
       art facilities that meet or exceed UC policy.

Philanthropy catalyzed the Berkeley Innovation Zone. The South Building will be developed by
a generous donor and provide facilities to help campus faculty and researchers, as well as
campus-affiliated entrepreneurs and their collaborators incubate startup companies that provide
solutions for energy and the environment. The building will be designed around highly flexible
wet laboratories and spaces allowing for scale-up capabilities to support startups rooted in
chemistry, materials science, and rapid instrument prototyping, propelled by artificial
intelligence and machine learning.

This item requests that the Regents approve design following action pursuant to the California
Environmental Quality for the South Building and the acceptance of the gift by the donor to UC
Berkeley for the South Building. This item also requests that the Regents approve the Berkeley
Innovation Zone Site Preparation project. The campus would return to the Regents to request
approval of the North Building project.


                                  PROJECT DESCRIPTION

The Berkeley Innovation Zone would be in Downtown Berkeley, immediately west of UC
Berkeley’s Campus Park on a Regents-owned parcel bounded by University Avenue to the north,
Oxford Street to the east, Addison Street to the south and other private developments to the west
(refer to Project Location Map, Attachment 3).

North and South Buildings

The Berkeley Innovation Zone would redevelop this block with new space for research and
collaboration in two new buildings – the South Building and the North Building. The buildings
would provide space for academic research and innovation in the life sciences and related fields,
including materials science, advanced machine learning, AI, and other fields, and include space
for offices and other collaborative meeting spaces. The buildings would house researchers,
faculty, and students from across multiple disciplines relevant to the life and climate sciences.
The South and North Buildings would be separated by a linear-shaped courtyard open space.
Table 1 summarizes the two buildings’ characteristics.

The South Building will provide an approximately 176,000-gross-square-foot (gsf) new
laboratory building.
    • The building will include wet and dry laboratory research space, laboratory support
       space, research and administrative offices, meeting rooms, collaboration areas, and
       conference space, and shared administrative support space.
    • The building will provide incubator space for campus faculty and researchers, as well as
       campus-affiliated entrepreneurs and their collaborators, to cultivate startup companies
       that provide solutions for energy and the environment.
    • The building will have five above-ground floors, a non-occupied mechanical space at the
       roof, and a below-grade basement.
    • The ground floor will include active ground-floor uses along the Addison and Oxford
       Street frontages.

The campus is evaluating implementation options for the North Building and will return to the
Regents later for approvals pursuant to Regents Policy 8103. The campus anticipates that the
North Building would be an approximately 310,000-gsf laboratory building.
   • The building would include wet and dry laboratory research and laboratory support
       space, research and administrative offices, meeting rooms, and conference space.
   • The building would include space for UC Berkeley’s Innovative Genomics Institute
       (IGI), as well as other organizations, such as government and non-governmental
       organizations, private companies, and research institutes with which UC Berkeley and/or
       IGI has an affiliation.
   • The building would have up to 11 above-ground floors and include a non-occupied
       mechanical space at the roof, a below-grade basement, and may also include an
       approximately 154,400-gsf garage, with up to four above-ground levels, comprising up to
       350 vehicle parking spaces, within the 11-floor building.
   • The building would include approximately 5,000 gsf of ground-floor commercial space
       for public-serving uses along the University Avenue and/or Oxford Street frontages.

Berkeley Innovation Zone Site Preparation Project

Prior to the development of the North and South Buildings, the campus would demolish
buildings and features on the site (University Hall, 2136-2140 University Avenue, and 2154-
2160 University Avenue) as part of the Berkeley Innovation Zone Site Preparation project.
University Hall is currently vacant and has a Seismic Performance Rating of VI. Tenants of
2136-2140 and 2154-2160 University would relocate prior to demolition.

Through demolition, the project will eliminate over $22 million of building and infrastructure
restoration and renewal needs identified via the Integrated Capital Asset Management Program
(ICAMP). The project also demolishes approximately 175,000 gsf of space that are not compliant
with seismic safety requirements.

                          PROJECT DESIGN – SOUTH BUILDING

The Berkeley Innovation Zone project components would redevelop the project block in a
manner that responds to the surrounding development context and optimizes site capacity at a
key campus gateway for new laboratory and research space (refer to Attachment 4, Design
Graphics). The project would be consistent with the general objectives of UC Berkeley’s 2021
Physical Development Framework (PhDF), particularly those related to enhancing the campus’s
western gateway entrances and complementing the surrounding private development context.
The South Building would be constructed on the southern portion of the Berkeley Innovation
Zone block along Addison Street near Oxford Street after completion of the Site Preparation
project.

The South Building would be approximately 100 feet above ground level at its southwest corner,
and 92 feet above ground level on its east side, at Oxford Street. An approximately 20-foot-high
non-occupied mechanical space would be located on the roof but would be set back from the
façade and would not be visible from the street level. The building form and organization would
be driven by the internal program and sustainability considerations. The primary entrance would
be from Addison Street, near Oxford Street, where collaboration and prototyping spaces would
be located. The southeast corner would feature a multi-story, semi-enclosed space featuring a
central stairwell. On level two, this space would provide access to an exterior terrace. Floors
three through five would feature a mix of flexible laboratory and office space. The building’s
east-west orientation optimizes natural light and solar exposure, provides opportunities for
passive ventilation systems, and optimizes the sawtooth roof form for photovoltaic power
generation. The building’s service and loading access would occur at the west end of the
building. The building’s façade would be a mix of glass and brick (refer to Attachment 4, Design
Graphics).

The South Building project would install streetscape features along its site perimeter, including

Long Range Development Plan

The project is consistent with the 2021 LRDP. The site was identified as a future building area
within UC Berkeley’s City Environs land use area, which allows for academic and research
functions as well as visitor serving functions (refer to Attachment 7). The project supports the
2021 LRDP’s objectives for prioritizing sites adjacent to the Campus Park for functions that
benefit from connectivity with the Campus Park; complementing and reinforcing surrounding
land uses, including those described City of Berkeley Downtown Area Plan; and ensuring the
highest and best use of campus land through demolition and redevelopment of seismically non-
compliant space for critically necessary research and collaboration space that supports the
academic strategic plan.


                            REAL PROPERTY TRANSACTIONS

Gift of Real Property – South Building

The donor has committed to designing and constructing the South Building Project and
subsequently making a gift of the South Building project to UC Berkeley. To effectuate the gift,
the donor and the Berkeley campus would execute a short-term Construction License granting
the donor the right to construct the building on Regents’ property. The Construction License
provides that (i) the donor would construct the South Building Project as agreed to and permitted
by UC Berkeley, (ii) the donor would be responsible for the entire cost of the planning, design,
and construction of the South Building project, and (iii) the donor would be responsible for
following all Regental and campus standards, policies, and procedural requirements applicable to
the South Building project. Further conditions of the gift agreement and a separate loan
agreement between a lender and the University include that:
       • the Berkeley campus is not committing to expend any of its own funds on the South
           Building or its embedded program.
       • No funds generated from the South Building will be used to repay the loan made for
           the purposes of the demolition of University Hall, so as not to trigger Unrelated
           Business Income Tax (UBIT).
       • The incubator program within the South Building will be self-sustaining and
           ultimately provide a cash surplus to be allocated as follows:
                 70 percent to be used for South Building activities at the discretion of the
                    South Building Project’s Advisory Board
                 20 percent for a campus-wide deferred maintenance fund
                 ten percent for the South Building Project’s Fellows program

 STATEMENT OF CONSISTENCY WITH SELECT UC POLICIES AND PRACTICE

The project is consistent with selected UC Policies and Practice:

Sustainable Practices Policy

This project will comply with the University of California Sustainable Practices Policy. The
Sustainable Practices Policy establishes goals for green building, clean energy, transportation,
climate protection, facilities operations, zero waste, procurement, food service, and water
systems. A full range of sustainability practices for building design and operations is included in
the budgeting, programming, and design effort for the project.

This project has been analyzed by the UC Operational Carbon and Energy Assessment for New
Construction (OCEAN) Tool and results are provided as Attachment 5. The OCEAN Tool
identifies high-level estimates of target site energy use, utility costs, and operational greenhouse
gas emissions for the proposed project. Building performance metrics are being compiled within
UC’s capital projects database and will be utilized to compare and assess future projects.

Small Business Enterprises (SBEs) and Disabled Veteran Business Enterprises (DVBEs)

The campus is committed to promoting and increasing participation of Small Business
Enterprises (SBEs) and Disabled Veteran Business Enterprises (DVBEs) in all purchasing and
contract business, subject to any applicable obligations under State and federal law, collective
bargaining agreements, and University policies. The Campus regularly communicates with
interested contractors and consultants to provide information about how to find opportunities
to work at the campus and to encourage them to respond to the annual announcement soliciting
interest to perform services. Providing qualified SBEs with the maximum opportunity to
participate will be encouraged with the selected design professionals and contractors with the
goal of meeting 25 percent participation.

Seismic Safety

This project will comply with the University of California Seismic Safety Policy, including
independent seismic peer review.
