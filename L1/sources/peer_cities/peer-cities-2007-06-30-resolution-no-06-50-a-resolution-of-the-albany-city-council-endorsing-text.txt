 1                                  RESOLUTION NO #06-50

 2      A RESOLUTION OF THE ALBANY CITY COUNCIL ENDORSING AND
     DIRECTING STAFF TO PROCEED WITH A CITY DIRECTED WATERFRONT
 3                        PLANNING PROCESS

 4           WHEREAS, the Albany Waterfront includes both privately held lands and lands

 5   owned by public agencies.

 6           WHEREAS, the lands owned by the public agencies are dedicated for open space

 7   and parkland recreational uses.
 8           WHEREAS, the privately held lands have been zoned for waterfront related uses,
 9   permitting Golden Gate Fields and other water related commercial uses.
10           WHEREAS, the racetrack has been in operation for about sixty years and the
11   property has not undergone significant changes in appearance or use over this time frame.
12           WHEREAS, approximately once every decade the community has focused
13   attention on land use policies and changes at the waterfront.
14           WHEREAS, over the last several decades, the community and City have made
15   no determinations to change the General Plan and zoning regulations for the waterfront
16   area.
17           WHEREAS, in the 1980s, when the property was owned by Catellus
18   Corporation, the City undertook an environmental review for the privately owned lands.
19   This review was processed under the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) as a
20   program EIR. The study included assessments of environmental impacts of potential
21   projects that ranged from a “park only” alternative to a build-out of approximately 4.2
22   million square feet of mixed use development. After preparation and certification of the
23

24

                                                  1
 1   EIR, Catellus did not submit any applications, but, instead, extended the lease to allow

 2   racing to continue. Thereafter, Catellus sold the property to Ladbroke.

 3          WHEREAS, in 1990, in part due to citizens’ concerns about changes in use and

 4   development at the waterfront, a citizen initiative (Measure C) was approved by the

 5   voters of the City of Albany. Measure C reserves to the voters the final approval of any

 6   changes in waterfront General Plan and zoning regulations. In passing Measure C, the

 7   voters determined that the waterfront is a unique community asset that is special.

 8          WHEREAS, in 1994 Ladbroke Racing proposed a change in use to allow for

 9   card room gaming at the racetrack. This proposal included provisions to provide the City

10   with additional revenues, a Bay Trail, funds to develop the Bay Trail, and other benefits.

11   The card room proposal was controversial. It narrowly won voter approval by the voters,

12   but was legally challenged and the voter approval was set aside.

13          WHEREAS, since 1994 and until recently, there has been no planning process

14   designed and completed to review present zoning regulations and to discuss potential

15   changes.

16          WHEREAS, within the last two years, the present owner of the private property,

17   Magna Entertainment, has expressed interest in exploring changes to the regulations that

18   apply to the Waterfront lands and most recently entered into a joint venture agreement

19   with Caruso Affiliated to prepare an application for a predominately retail development

20   on the waterfront

21          WHEREAS, the Albany Shoreline Specific Plan Initiative, if approved by the

22   voters, would require the City to establish a special task force to prepare a specific plan

23   in accordance with specified guidelines and procedures. The specific plan that would be

24

                                                  2
 1   created under the initiative would have to follow specified policies and guidelines and

 2   would not allow for community viewpoints differing from those policies or guidelines to

 3   be considered.

 4          WHEREAS, a process that provides technical analysis and community dialogue

 5   on a full spectrum of possible land use configurations is the most desirable way to

 6   address the competing factors that arise when contemplating significant regulatory

 7   changes at the waterfront.

 8          WHEREAS, City staff recommends to the City Council that it authorize a City

 9   planning process in a format to be developed by the Planning and Zoning Commission in

10   consultation with other City commissions and committees.

11          WHEREAS, Caruso Affiliated representatives and Albany staff have discussed

12   for several months formulating a process that includes review of the Caruso project in

13   conjunction with a City planning process designed to study other alternative proposals

14   appropriate for the Albany Waterfront area;

15          WHEREAS, a joint process would have included the preparation of an EIR to

16   analyze the Caruso project along with other alternatives:

17          WHEREAS, Caruso Affiliated recently indicated in a letter dated July 7, 2006

18   that it believed the proposed City process might create added legal exposure to Caruso

19   Affiliated if the Caruso project was adopted;

20          WHEREAS, Caruso Affiliated requested that the Albany City Council adopt a

21   resolution prepared by Caruso that would require the City Council to agree to the

22   preparation of an EIR for their project at this time prior to even submitting its

23   application;

24

                                                   3
 1          WHEREAS, the City Council indicated that it was not comfortable with

 2   approving the Caruso drafted resolution prior to the submission of an application in order

 3   to understand and to review the types of approvals being requested;

 4          WHEREAS, the City Council wishes to assure Caruso Affiliated that the City

 5   would process a Caruso Affiliated application in accordance with City standard

 6   processing protocols and in conformity with the laws and regulations of the City of

 7   Albany and the State of California;

 8          WHEREAS, staff has summarized to the City Council the basic steps included in

 9   the standard manner in which applications are reviewed;

10          NOW, THEREFORE, THE ALBANY CITY COUNCIL DOES HEREBY

11   RESOLVE, DECLARE, DETERMINE, AND ORDER AS FOLLOWS:

12          1.      The community would be best served if the City commenced its own

13   planning process;

14          2.      A range of viewpoints for the future of the waterfront would be included

15   in a City-initiated planning process, including the property owner, potential developer,

16   proponents of the initiative, and other community members;

17          3.      Staff is authorized to identify funding for the costs of the City-initiated

18   planning process;
