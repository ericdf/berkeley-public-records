SUMMARY
The Human Resources Department is proposing a total budget reduction of approximately 13%,
for a total reduction of $668,804 achieved through:

      •   Personnel elimination of two vacant Workers’ Compensation (WC) funded positions and
          cost reallocation of $433,037
      •   General Fund non-personnel reductions of $238,236
This proposal reflects a deliberate effort to preserve core HR service delivery while aligning with
the City’s fiscal constraints and stewarding public funds responsibly. The reductions rely
primarily on structural adjustments and contract eliminations rather than one-time savings or
reductions in filled positions.
I. Personnel Total: $433,037
Positions Eliminated (Vacant):

      •   HR Associate Analyst (WC) – PCN 1195
      •   Occupational Health & Safety Specialist – PCN 4764
                        Combined Budgeted Salary: $244,695
                         Fringe (FY26 rate 0.7697): $188,342
                                              Total: $433,037
These positions are currently vacant and fully funded by the Workers’ Compensation Fund.
Rather than filling these vacancies, we propose reallocating existing General Fund HR staff time

to perform Workers’ Compensation functions and thereby cost shift an equivalent amount of
existing GF personnel costs to the WC Fund.
This action does not increase overall WC Fund spending. It reallocates existing capacity within
the fund to the GF personnel that will absorb the work because of eliminating the two WC
positions.
Operational Impact:
High.
Workers’ Compensation case management and safety/occupational health functions will
continue but will be absorbed by existing HR staff. As a result, capacity for program
development and initiatives beyond baseline compliance will be reduced.
The existing Safety Officer and Senior Human Resources Analyst (Workers’ Compensation) will
no longer have dedicated operational redundancy staffing. To mitigate this reduced backup
capacity, identified staff absorbing the work via this cost shift will require extensive cross-
training to ensure continuity of operations and the ability to prioritize this work to cover
vacations, unexpected leaves, or vacancies as they arise.
For occupational health and safety the capacity to take proactive measures is significantly
reduced. This includes capacity and response times for non-baseline initiatives like ergonomic
assessments and site visits.
For worker’s compensation and leaves, HR will closely monitor case closure timelines, claim
progression, and internal tracking metrics to ensure the City does not incur additional costs due
to processing delays resulting from this shift in staffing structure. If performance indicators
suggest that delays create increased claim exposure or financial impact, HR will reassess
workload distribution and reprioritize assigned staff duties as necessary to ensure this cost-
shifting proposal results in a net savings to the City.
II. General Fund Non-Personnel Reductions Total: $238,236
1. Employer of Choice Consulting – $96,742
This eliminates external consulting support focused on employer branding, workforce strategy,
and retention positioning.
Operational Impact: Low.
Strategic employer branding efforts will be reduced. Recruitment marketing enhancements will
be internally managed with reduced capacity. Longer-term workforce competitiveness
initiatives may slow. In the near term these initiatives may not be a City priority as potential
reductions in force loom.
2. HR Accuity Software Cancellation – $46,494
This software tool was purchased for Employee and Labor Relations case tracking.
Operational Impact: Low to none.
                                                                                                  2
HR staff did not fully adopt or integrate the software into daily operations. Case tracking and
documentation will continue through established internal systems utilizing the Microsoft Suite
and other existing tools, which remain sufficient for operational needs.
There had been interest expressed by the City Attorney’s Office in using the platform to
monitor HR case activity. While interdepartmental transparency and collaboration are
important, a review of system license activity and login history indicates that City Attorney staff
have not accessed or utilized the platform in several months an even then only minimally and
sporadically.
Given the limited adoption and absence of active cross-departmental utilization, discontinuing
the software represents a fiscally responsible reduction without material operational impact.
3. GovInvest Contract Elimination – $45,000
This contract provided advanced financial modeling tools to support long-term forecasting of
personnel costs, retirement liabilities, and scenario modeling for labor negotiations.
Operational Impact: Medium to low.
Eliminating this contract will reduce access to modeling tools. Long-range personnel cost
modeling will instead require closer collaboration between HR and Budget using existing
internal tools and spreadsheets.
While this approach remains viable, it will increase manual analysis time and reduce the speed
at which complex compensation and liability scenarios can be evaluated. The City may
experience longer turnaround times for detailed cost projections, particularly during active
bargaining cycles.
4. Commercial Driver’s Program Recordkeeping Contract – $50,000
Reduction of contract services for document storage and commercial driver’s license tracking.
The work will be absorbed internally.
Operational Impact: None.
This budget object code was increased in anticipation of adoption of a Commercial Driver’s
Program software implementation. The planned Request for Proposals will be cancelled. The
work will continue to be absorbed by existing staff across HR and Public Works (Safety Officer
classification).
