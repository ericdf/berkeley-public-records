O ice of Economic Development (OED) | Community Development Project Coordinator (CDPC)
Position Currently held by Vincent McCoy

The Community Development Project Coordinator (CDPC) serves as a critical operational
catalyst within the O ice of Economic Development (OED), directly supporting the business
community, driving the city's commercial district vitality, small business resilience, and
commercial corridor health. By managing essential merchant relations, facilitating cross-
departmental policy implementation, and serving as a bridge between the city and local business
communities, this role directly translates into measurable local impact.

CDPC - Functional Task Breakdown & Workload Allocation

Commercial District Support & Governance

      Workload Allocation: 35% of total time (~14 hours/week).

      Primary Impact: Serves as the primary point of contact for ﬁve business areas: Lorin, North
       Shattuck, Telegraph, Solano, and Elmwood.

      Key Responsibilities: Regularly attends board meetings, supports community events, and
       interfaces directly with local merchants and property owners. Fulﬁlls formal city
       administrative governance mandates by serving as the Commission Secretary for both the
       Solano and Elmwood districts, contributing to the Telegraph BID Board of Directors and
       serving on the Executive Committee, and routinely analyzing district ﬁnancial statements to
       ensure ﬁscal health of our city’s Business Improvement Districts (BIDs) and merchant
       organizations.

Business Assistance, Permitting & Inquiries

      Workload Allocation: 25% of total time (~10 hours/week).

      Primary Impact: Functions as a front-line communications and navigation hub for
       Berkeley's business and commercial real estate sectors.

      Key Responsibilities: Directs the resolution of inquiries to OED communication channels
       (email and phone), resolves 5–6 substantial and often complex inquiries per week. Manages
       a steady pipeline of 2–3 real estate queries weekly from both internal divisions like Health,
       Housing, and Community Services (HHCS) and external partners (small businesses, non-
       proﬁt organizations, solo entrepreneurs, etc.) seeking to get established or expand in
       Berkeley. Provides hands-on technical support for 4–5 cases each week (that may come in
       from other sources including council districts, merchant organizations, business events,
       etc.), which can include assisting individual small business owners directly, as well as
       commercial brokerage ﬁrms like Norheim & Yost and Gordon Commercial alongside their
       clients as they look for suitable locations to get established in Berkeley.

Financial Programs & Contract Management

      Workload Allocation: 15% of total time (~6 hours/week).

      Primary Impact: Secures small business longevity and operational continuity through
       formal ﬁnancial stewardship and strategic third-party program management.

      Key Responsibilities: Directs the administrative framework of the city's Revolving Loan
       Fund (RLF) and Resiliency Loan Programs (RLP), serving as the Secretary for the Loan
       Administration Board. Coordinates with the Finance Department to compile and verify
       essential yearly audit documentation related to the loan funds. Supervises external
       compliance and outcomes for critical small business technical assistance contracts,
       including partnerships with Working Solutions (for the management of the loan funds) and
       Project Equity for employee-ownership and business succession planning assistance.

   Data Analysis & Event Production

      Workload Allocation: 15% of total time (~6 hours/week, seasonal).

      Primary Impact: Powers citywide economic intelligence tracking and supports high-
       visibility economic development events.

      Key Responsibilities: Tracks local economic indicators by designing, coordinating and
       executing ﬁeld collection protocols for commercial district inventory (storefront vacancy
       data), performing complex sales tax analyses, producing employment data metrics, and
       producing the city’s published economic dashboard. Conceptualizes, plans, supports and
       assists with executing major annual economic events, including the Small Business Forum
       and Manufacturing Week.

Interdepartmental Policy & Strategic Research Support

      Workload Allocation: 10% of total time (~4 hours/week).

      Primary Impact: Aligns cross-departmental operations and delivers rapid-response policy
       research to support active City Council and mayoral legislative priorities.

      Key Responsibilities: Conducts quick-turnaround policy research and stakeholder
       outreach on sensitive, evolving council directives. Provides support and assistance (and an
       economic development/small business perspective) in consultation with other city
       departments on work initiatives. Recent examples include, working with the Planning
       Department on corridor zoning changes, collaborating with Code Enforcement on
       implementing vacant storefront accountability programs, and working with the Zero Waste
       division to deploy commercial infrastructure, including street-level trash receptacles on
       Euclid Avenue’s commercial district, and o ering education to merchants on embracing
       reusable food ware initiatives.
