The Finance Department has been operating for years at only the very minimum staffing
levels that allow us to perform our key functions: (a) processing the City’s cash receipts;
(b) billing and collecting tax and fee revenues; (c) procurement of the goods and services
for critical City operations; (d) ensuring key controls to protect City assets; and (e)
ensuring proper accounting and financial reporting for City management, bond
investors, and required statutory and regulatory oversight.

To help address the City’s current fiscal crisis, and at the direction of City leadership, we
have proposed certain cost cutting measures resulting in the elimination of certain
General Fund positions. While we recognize that changes must be made to bring
expenses in line with recurring revenues, cutting these positions will have dire
consequences for our operations and our ability to provide services to other
departments, City residents and all our mandated requirements.

Some of the specific impacts are:

Eliminating the Systems Accountant position will result in a vastly decreased ability to
internally troubleshoot technical and other issues with our financial accounting and
reporting system (ERMA). Without this internal support, the City will have to engage
outside consultants, increasing timelines, expense, and impacting the continuity of
Finance and other City financial operations. This position regularly resolves system
issues and advises users, and response times from software vendors (Tyler and others)
can be delayed by days or more. Decreased assistance will be provided to ERMA users
across all City departments for all financial transactions. There will also be decreased
Citywide support for other financial systems and implementation projects. The Systems
Accountant plays a key role as a bridge between the financial operations subject matter
experts and the information technology support of all financial systems. There will be
less innovation and fewer improvements to payment acceptance and collections
systems - potentially impacting revenues. Finally, cutting this position will cause longer
upgrade cycles and increase costs for outside consulting support, and there will be an
increased burden on the Department of Information Technology to scope projects,
gather requirements, and assess resources needed for projects that involve billings,
payments, or otherwise interface with other City financial systems.

Without the Senior Systems Analyst, the department will have a much more limited
ability to help manage financial system implementation projects. This position was
created to manage the assignment of implementation tasks to department staff, to
ensure efficient progress toward system innovation while preserving continuity of
ongoing operations. It allows Finance to implement financial systems with significantly
less project management resources needed from the IT Department. Since it was
recently vacated, the pace of implementation has slowed dramatically. Finance has
many future projects: Replacement of FUND$ property tax assessment; a new Business
License Tax system; transferring General Billing and Cash Receipting to ERMA from
FUND$; a new Land Management system to replace FUND$; a Collections Management
system; a Miscellaneous Tax assessment and collections system (Hotel, Parking Lot,
Utility Users, and others); and the expansion of online payment options for City
customers. Cutting this position leaves significantly less capacity for innovations in
financial systems, as the IT Department does not have knowledge of accounting and
financial operations needed to effectively support and plan these projects.

Other than an Assistant Management Analyst position which is restricted by its funding
sources to working on Property Tax assessment and billing, this Associate Management
Analyst is the only analyst in the Finance Department. Without this position, the
department will have to reduce the volume of analysis that can be performed internally,
and will add to the workload of divisional managers, who will have to reduce support and
oversight of their respective teams. Another result will be a decreased ability to report
out revenue details for specific streams and analyze receivable balances, which could
hamper collections activities and lead to reduced revenues for both General Fund and
the Zero Waste Fund. This position also currently serves as a backup escalation point
when the Revenue Collection Manager is unavailable, and so without it, both staff and
City customers, residents and other taxpayers will see reductions in support.

The elimination of two Revenue Development Specialist positions will result in
significantly reduced capacity for auditing businesses to recover unreported and
underreported taxes. There will also be lower collections efforts and larger receivables
balances going uncollected due to statutory limitations on the timeframe of collections
efforts which may be pursued on delinquent customer accounts.

A reduction of one Senior Accountant will impose an increased workload on an already
overburdened accounting staff, decreasing morale and making resignations more likely.
It would require the Accounting Manager to assume many duties of this Senior
Accountant, and to abide by internal controls requiring segregation of certain duties, it
would also push a great deal of work to the Department Director. The analysis and
implementation of GASB pronouncements will need to be done by external consultants.
There will be an increased risk to the City’s ability to complete the annual financial audit
and federal grants Single Audit by prescribed deadlines, as well as impacts to the
timeliness of other statutory reporting requirements. Ultimately, this could cause
damage to the City's reputation for financial management and lead to higher borrowing
cost if bond ratings are affected.

In short, the reduction of these positions will add risk the efficiency and safety of the
City’s financial operations, while crippling our capacity for innovation in the services we
provide to City administration and the public at large.
