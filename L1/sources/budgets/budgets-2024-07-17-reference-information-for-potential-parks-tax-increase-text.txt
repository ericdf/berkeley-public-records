On December 12, 2023 the Parks Recreation and Waterfront (PRW) Commission
submitted a report recommending that the City Council include a Parks Tax Increase of
4ct/ft2 or ~20%, in the November 2024 ballot and that funding be identified to include
this measure in a future community survey.
This report identifies three areas where additional Parks Tax funding is needed:
        1) Unequal Funding Across Parks: Parks and landscape maintenance in the
           Waterfront be funded by the Parks Tax, instead of the Marina Fund.
        2) Declining Urban Forest: The formation of a permanent city-wide Tree Planting
           Unit
        3) Unfunded Major Maintenance and Capital Liabilities: Adding additional
           funding for PRW capital improvement projects (CIP).
As the City Council considers the PRW Commission’s request, this document is meant
to provide background and reference information. Attachment 1 details revenues
associated with potential 18%, 20% and 28% increases to the Parks Tax.
BACKGROUND
        Parks Tax History

The Parks Tax was established in 1997, replacing the Landscape Maintenance Tax.
This parcel tax was amended in 2014 with 75% voter approval to increase the per
square foot tax rate from $0.1256 to $0.1466 (approximately a 17% increase) and add
an inflation escalator option, such that the yearly increase can be tied to either the
Consumer Price Index (CPI) or Personal Income Growth (PIG), whichever is greatest.

At the time this increase avoided the layoff of 4-5 Parks Tax funded staff and set aside
over $1.2M for CIP and minor maintenance. The Parks Tax is generally healthy, but
increased CIP expenditures because of a reduction in General Fund CIP in FY24 and
the transfer of some traditional Marina Fund operational costs will leave the fund
balance at 8% by the end of FY26 and less than 1% by the end of FY27. The current
parcel tax rate is $0.213 per square foot and the anticipated FY25 revenue is
approximately $18,298,000.

       Marina Fund History

The Marina Fund (MF) was established in 1964 as a requirement related to a State
Boating & Waterways (DBW) loan to build the Marina. Since that time nearly all
revenues and expenditures for Waterfront operations and most of the capital
improvement projects have run through the Marina Fund. The main revenues
supporting this fund are from berthers/slip holders and restaurant and hotel lease
agreements. Both of these funding sources are largely the result of non-resident
participants as an estimated less than 7% of slip holders are Berkeley residents, and
likely fewer are guests of the DoubleTree Hotel.

The MF balance has been challenging to keep positive and by the late 1990s showed
signs of distress. For decades slip holder fees were the lowest in the region, while no
funding was set aside for Waterfront infrastructure improvements. An operational deficit
has existed since 2008 because of the failure of much of the original infrastructure,
safety concerns that developed in the Waterfront and COVID-19. Since 2008, the City
has kept the Fund in the positive by reducing full-time positions, reducing Marina Fund
spending on CIP and major maintenance, and most recently buttressing the Fund with
COVID relief (American Rescue Plan) funds and General Fund. General Fund support
has been needed since 2021 to maintain Waterfront operations when the MF balance
finally evaporated. The Council authorized transfers to the MF of $1.4M in FY22 and
$1.15M in FY23 using American Rescue Plan funding to keep the MF solvent. In FY24,
Council authorized more than $800k in one-time cost shifts off the MF, as well as an
internal loan for the D & E Dock Replacement Project that saved the MF 30 years of
debt service costs of $385k/year. In order to stay in the positive in FY25 Marina Fund
received a partial “holiday” for indirect costs ($159K) and Workers Compensation
expenditures ($198K) and costs shifts to the Parks Tax ($347K) that was generated by
the Workers Compensation fund “holiday” for a total of $704K. In FY26 the anticipated
gap in the Marina Fund will be $968k if these balancing measures do not continue.
The operational gap is trending down as it was $2.1M in FY23, $1.5M in FY24, and is
projected to be $1.4M in FY25, $968K in FY26 and $650K in FY27. Marina slip
occupancy has risen for the last 2 years to 86% (from 78% in 2020), and is expected to
continue to increase with multiple capital improvements underway. Additional revenue is

DISCUSSION
       Costs that Can be Covered by Either the Marina Fund or Parks Tax

The Marina Fund has always paid for operational costs associated with the Waterfront,
including parks and landscaping costs, despite these costs being eligible for the Parks
Tax. Staff estimate that approximately $1.55M (See Attachment 2) of Waterfront costs
are associated with parks, park buildings and landscaping and therefore Parks Tax-
eligible. In FY24, City Council authorized the use of one-time Parks Tax funds to pay for
2 positions in the Waterfront. Moving forward, this will be more difficult as the fund
balance of the Parks Tax will only be 8% by the end of FY26.

The PRW Commission recommendation cites how a voter-approved tax measure to
increase the Parks Tax could unburden the Marina Fund and eliminate its structural
deficit. Additionally, as the deficit continues to shrink, these funds could help
supplement unfunded projects in the Waterfront (see Attachment 3).
       PRW Urban Forestry Tree Planting History: Need for Dedicated Staff

Since the establishment of the Parks Tax, the Urban Forestry Unit has primarily used
State grant funds to purchase trees and tree planting supplies, while using Parks Tax-
funded positions to plant trees city-wide. Urban Forestry staff typically dedicate time
during the late Winter and early Spring to tree planting, while breaking from work on
pruning and removals to plant about 200 new trees in parks, medians and sidewalk right
of way each year.

Because of the recent drought conditions resulting in the death of many City trees, the
aging of the City’s urban forest and the need for Fire Fuel reduction in the certain areas
of the City, City staff has had to remove significant numbers of trees. These removals,
which have resulted in increased work, combined with the threat of negative effects
relate to climate change, have made it important for the City to plant more trees.
Additionally, in the last eight years many State and Federal grants have focused on
providing tree planting funds to only plant areas with low tree canopy. The City has
received four of these grants since 2016. In 2018 staff started dedicating $75,000 of
Parks Tax CIP funds to plant trees in areas of the City not covered by State and Federal
grants.

The need to plant more trees, the increased work due to climate change (more tree
removals and increase in the amount of required young tree care) and the limitation of
funds for tree planting has resulted in the need to establish a Tree Planting Unit that is
in addition to the Urban Forestry staff who prune and remove trees. In FY24, Council
authorized a new position to supervise tree planting activities funded by the Parks Tax.
The PRW Department has been able to hire two new project-based Landscape
Gardener positions with grant funds. With these three new positions, PRW has planted
over 800 trees in 2024. Unfortunately, these project-based positions have been difficult
to keep filled because they are temporary and the grant funding will soon expire. Moving
forward, the goal is to establish career positions and budget that are dedicated to
planting trees throughout the City to replace our aging urban forest, ensure a high
number of tree plantings and a high frequency of watering each year, and have funding
for match requirements more potential grants.
       Annual PRW Capital Improvement Project Funding History

Parks, Recreation and Waterfront Department (PRW) annual/baseline Capital
Improvement Project (PRW Baseline CIP) and major maintenance funding has
historically come from the CIP Fund, Parks Tax, Marina Fund and the Camps Fund.
Other funding sources such as grants, insurance claims, General Fund, Bayer Fund and
developer agreements have also contributed to CIP project funding (See Attachment 4
for a list of over $130M in non-City funding sources). Additionally, the 2016 Measure T1
Bond Measure has contributed approximately $50M to PRW CIP.

Annual GF (CIP) Fund Contributions to CIP
From 2000 to 2003 the annual CIP contribution was $750,000. By 2007 the allocation
was reduced to $400,000: $200,000 for both ADA and playground safety improvements.
This changed in the FY23 and FY24 budget cycle, when PRW Baseline CIP funding
was increased to $2,000,000 in FY23 and $2,400,000 in FY24.

Annual Parks Tax Fund Contributions to PRW CIP
Since the early 2000s, the Parks Tax contributed $250,000 per year to PRW Baseline
CIP. When Measure F passed in 2014 this allocation rose to $1,000,000. Since then,
the allocation has risen slightly to $1,065,000 and will be $1,090,000 in FY26.

Annual Marina Fund Contributions to PRW CIP
Since the early 2000s, the Marina Fund has contributed between $250,000- $450,000
per year to PRW Baseline CIP. The contribution in FY25 and FY26 is $350,000/year.

Annual Camps Fund Contributions to PRW CIP
From 2006 through 2013, when the Rim Fire destroyed Berkeley Tuolumne Camp, the
Camps Funds contributed $80,000-$200,000 of annual funding to the PRW Baseline

Parks, Recreation and Waterfront Department (PRW)
Highest CIP Priorities- FY25

Below is a list of PRW’s unfunded highest priority emergency projects. This $20.5M list is a subset of the
departments $225M unfunded list.

Aquatic Park Tide Tube Maintenance/Replacement Project - $4.0M
The eight tide tubes servicing Aquatic Park need immediate replacement or sleeving. Staff have applied
for a planning grant from the Federal PROTECT Grant that was not funded. Staff are submitting another
grant to fund both the planning and construction of this project in September. City Council authorized
an $800,000 set aside for the matching total needed (20%) for this project in FY24.

South Cove Seawall Replacement- $5.25M
This project received 50% of the needed planning funding ($250,000) in the FY25 budget process. Staff
are issuing the contract for these planning funds now and will begin public process in October.

Glendale-La Loma Park 2-5, 5-12 Playground replacement - $1.06M
Current 2-5/5-12 play structures are not accessible and many of the 4 x 4 wood standards have failed.

Codornices Park 2-5, 5-12 Playground Replacement $1.06M
Current 2-5/5-12 play structures are not accessible and many of the 4 x 4 wood standards have failed.

Aquatic Park (Dreamland) Play Structure Replacement ($2.5M or $4.5M)
$300,000 in planning funds have been identified to design this 2-12 year- old play structure and the
adjacent area. The designer is on board and the public process will begin this fall. This project will
dovetail with a Public Works Strom drain/bioswale project. Additionally, if funding allows, project could
include new parking, restroom and pathway reorganization.

Shorebird Park 2-12 Play Structure Replacement ($790,000)
Current wood standards are failing and needs replacement.

South Berkeley Landscaping (Here/There)- $400,000
This project includes landscaping around the BART entrance in South Berkeley (Here/There area), Bus
lab triangle and the area at the south end of the BART station. Council authorized $75,000 for planning,
design and public process

Echo Lake Camp ADA, Phase 2- $2.3M
Phase 1 is funded and will be complete by the end of October. Phase 2 is designed, but needs $2.3M to
construct.

Santa Fe ROW Project - $3.150M
This railroad track to park project has $5M in a State grant funds, but needs another $3.15M to
complete the 4-block park. The toxins in the 4-block area were significantly worse than expected and are
requiring extra funding to clean up. Staff unsuccessfully applied for the gap ($3.15M) from a separate
Federal grant in 2023. We plan on applying again later this year for the same Federal grant. While this
project is not an emergency, if we don’t complete the original scope of this project by finding the
additional funding by FY27, the City risks losing are $5.0M of State grant funding.
