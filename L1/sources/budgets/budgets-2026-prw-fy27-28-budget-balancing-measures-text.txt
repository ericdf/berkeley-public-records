PRW FY27-28 Budget Balancing Measures



General Fund 12.5% Reductions and Impacts

•   Youthworks Division: There will be 39 fewer youth internships, reducing the summer
    program from 142 to 103 internships for Berkeley residents with a focus on historically
    underserved communities. Summer internships had already decreased from 267 in
    2024 to 142 in 2025 when the budget was right-sized. This reduces the City’s ability to
    meet high community need, with more than 500 applicants in 2025, and more than 600
    applicants expected in 2026.

•   Recreation Division:

       •   Reductions of 4.0 FTE (Including the AFS that is being held vacant from FY26)
           This elimination of 14% of Recreation programming staff combined with the 2.46
           FTE cost shifts to the Camps Fund and Parks Tax Fund, will require a division-
           wide reorganization, further reductions in programs due to lack of supervision
           and coordination, and realignment of existing budget to meet the need for
           increased scholarships due to cuts in programs designed for low-income
           participants.

       •   Projected impacts of program reductions due to division-wide staffing cuts
           include:

              •   Partial pools closure (weekly or seasonal). This could be closing on
                  weekends, or one weekend day, at one or both pools. Currently, on
                  average, 172 patrons visit King Pool and 124 patrons visit West Campus
                  Pool each weekend. Examples of weekend programs are lap swim,
                  independent exercise, senior exercise, community/family swim, and
                  swim lessons. Alternatively, one pool could be closed during the winter
                  season. This was the case prior to 2019, when West Campus Pool
                  opened only during summer months. This past year, we had 12,106 visits
                  to West Campus Pool during the off-season.

              •   Elimination of Therapeutic Recreation (special needs) programming.
                  Currently, our TR program provides additional support for up to 21 youth
                  with special needs to participate in summer camp and afterschool
                  programs.

              •   50 % Reduction of Teen Friday night drop-in program, trips and special
                  events. Currently we offer drop-in every Friday night, Teen trips
           throughout the year to Echo Lake, Six Flags/Great America, Santa Cruz
           Boardwalk, Cal games, and the Black College Expo; We also host special
           events like the Teen Flashlight Egg-Hunt, Teen Dances, and Middle School
           Pool Parties; Over 1000 teens attended these events over the last year.

       •   50% Reduction of family-oriented/DEI special events. PRW hosts more
           than 80 special events each year, ranging from the Harvest Festival and
           Bay Festival, to Music and Movies in the Park, the Community Picnic,
           Skate Night, Game Nights, Live Oak Laughs, and Mothers’ Day Hats and
           High Tea. An estimated 20-25,000 people attend these events.

       •   Reduction of Tots and Pre-K programs. PRW provides a variety of
           popular programs for families with children under 5, including summer
           and spring break camps, Shooting Star Preschool, Wee-Play Drop In, and
           music camps. An estimated 150 participants per week attend drop-in
           programs; and 168 participants attend pre-k camp programs throughout
           the year.

       •   Reduction of Summer Camp and Afterschool Program slots. With
           reductions in career staffing, summer camp slots may need to be
           reduced by 36 to ensure appropriate supervision. Similarly, 36 slots per
           week would need to be reduced from afterschool programs (this
           represents 12 slots at each location between Frances Albrier, Live Oak
           and James Kenney.

       •   Reduction of Community Center hours. Career staff are responsible for
           scheduling, programming and supervising part-time staff so that
           community centers can stay open in the evenings and some weekends.
           Reductions will include hour s after 6pm, traditionally our centers are
           open till 9 or 10 pm.

•   Program reductions create the need for additional scholarships. As an
    example, closure of the City’s Playground Program, which is fully subsidized by
    the General Fund, would displace 72 participants each week during the summer.
    The Playground Program is our low-cost summer camp option where
    participants pay $10/week to attend. Those 72 families would still need to ﬁnd
    care for their children, and would likely need scholarships to participate in
    higher-cost City programs. This cycle of subsidized program closures increasing
    demand for scholarships for other programs will likely lead to pressures to
    increase the budget for scholarships and reduce eligibility thresholds.
•   Shower Program: The program currently operates at West Campus Pool 7 days/week,
    open to the public 1 hour/day. This reduction will result in program closure approx. 1
    day/week (43 days/year). This reduces access to opportunities for basic hygiene for the
    unhoused community.

•   Summer Lunch: The program currently runs at 4 sites, distributing free lunches and
    snacks. Budget reductions will lead to the elimination of 1 site. This creates a higher
    burden for community members to access free meals, and provides fewer meals and
    snacks to those who need them. This would affect an estimated 30 people per day each
    day of the summer.

•   Fire Fuel: PRW currently receives $450k/year to remove hazardous trees and
    vegetation. These balancing measures would reduce ﬁre fuel budget by $243k to close
    PRW’s budget gap. This work could be funded by FF but has remained GF over the last
    several years because there are other high priorities for FF funds. In this last budget
    cycle, PRW removed an estimated 210 tons in ﬁre fuel debris from the Berkeley hills.

•   Administration: General Fund is currently used to advertise PRW programs and events,
    produce ribbon cuttings, groundbreakings, and department-wide trainings. Balancing
    measure will result in lower-quality public engagement and elimination of all-staff
    department trainings and. These will result in less community awareness and
    understanding of our work, and lower staff morale and knowledge of City priorities.
