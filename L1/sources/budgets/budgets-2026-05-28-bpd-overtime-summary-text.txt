Subject:    BPD Overtime Summary


Summary
The department's overtime (OT) budget baseline has remained essentially flat over the last
3 fiscal years at approximately $7.5 million while annual actuals have climbed to
approximately $10 million. The structural gap is concentrated in the Communications
Center where scheduled dispatcher staffing sits below the minimum required to keep the
floor covered, making OT a necessity rather than discretionary. Sworn officer OT tracks
budget closely once reimbursements are accounted for, and it is anticipated that
negotiations with BPA will further help the Department manage Sworn officer OT. Targeted
operational investments outlined separately (Communications Center technology and the
public safety technology packages) would allow minimum staffing requirements to lower
and thereby relieve OT pressure and could reduce the size of future OT allocations.


Cost Growth in OT is Partly Mechanical
Even setting operational drivers aside, OT cost rises year over year through factors that
have nothing to do with hours worked: MOU-negotiated salary adjustments, step
increases, and premium pay additions all push the dollar cost of each OT hour upward. The
OT budget baseline has not been appropriately adjusted in the last few budget cycles for
these changes, which mechanically widens the budget-vs.-actual gap each year
independent of operations.


Scheduled Staffing vs. Minimum Staffing
OT is fundamentally a function of the buffer between how many staff are scheduled and
the minimum (agreed upon by BPD and BPA) required to safely deliver service. When
scheduled staffing exceeds the minimum, normal absences (vacation, sick, training, comp
time) can be absorbed without generating OT. When scheduled staffing equals or falls
below minimum staffing levels, every absence results in OT. The table below provides an

The budget for dispatch OT assumes full staffing. We have not had full Communications
Center staffing in many years, and scheduled staffing currently sits below the minimum
required to operate the Center, which means there is no buffer at all and every absence
converts directly into OT. In FY25, 86% of dispatch OT was directly attributed to backfill for
short staffing, while another 5% was attributed to backfill for training and sick leave, and
the remainder resulting from events, recruiting. Because much of dispatch OT is generated
from positions sitting vacant, the salary savings from those vacancies are converted nearly
1-for-1 into OT, meaning the unit can come in near its overall personnel budget while still
generating more OT than what has been budgeted.


BPA Negotiations
Provisions affecting how OT is generated and managed on the sworn side are actively being
discussed in current labor negotiations with the Berkeley Police Association, and the
department is making progress toward contract changes that could further moderate
sworn OT cost drivers going forward.


Next Steps
The goal in both Patrol and Dispatch is the same: widen the buffer between scheduled
staffing and the minimum required to deliver service, so that routine absences can be
absorbed without generating OT.
On the sworn side, that buffer has been maintained despite reduced staffing by reducing
beats and efficiently redistributing patrol resources. It can be widened through force-
multiplying technology- Drone as First Responder and UAS capabilities, continued ALPR

investment, and the surveillance camera program- all of which allow effective coverage of
the city at lower minimum staffing levels. The public safety technology package
contemplated separately is directly aligned with this approach and would, over time, allow
the OT allocation itself to be reduced rather than just held flat.
On the dispatch side, no operational fix substitutes for closing the vacancy gap. The single
highest-leverage action available is bringing Communications Center scheduled staffing
above the minimum threshold. The Communications Center technology package
contemplated separately (script-based dispatching software, automated non-emergency
call taking) is the direct lever here: once scheduled staffing exceeds minimum staffing, the
structural conversion of salary savings into OT ends, and dispatch OT can return to a
budget-aligned level.
Taken together, proposed technology investments target the actual mechanics of the
overage rather than the symptom, and they create the conditions under which the
department's future OT allocation can be reduced from its current level.
