South Cove West Lot Parking & Dock
Improvements Ribbon Cutting
We’re excited to share several important improvements at the Berkeley
Waterfront!

Renovations at the South Cove West Parking Lot include the installation of a
new storm water treatment system, along with enhanced safety features
designed to significantly improve both vehicle and pedestrian circulation.
These upgrades include new traffic islands, wheel stops, safety striping, and
updated signage to create a safer and more accessible environment for
everyone.

At the South Cove East Dock, critical pier piling repairs have restored the
structural integrity of the timber piles, strengthening the dock and allowing
it to safely reopen to recreational users.

The South Cove Middle Dock ADA gangway float, originally repaired in
2024, sustained irreparable damage during recent storm events and was




                                                     1
closed in late 2024. A new replacement float was installed, and the Middle
ADA Dock has now reopened to the public.

We look forward to celebrating these improvements with residents and
visitors at the Berkeley Waterfront and continuing to enhance this special
community space for all to enjoy. Please join us April 2, 2026, at 11 am
at the South Cove West parking lot the for a ribbon cutting and light
refreshments.




South Cove West Parking Lot Improvements


South Adeline Improvements: Dog Park,
Landscaping Improvements and Miyawaki
Forest
These projects introduce landscaping improvements to beautify and activate
the South Adeline area. Improvements include new planting and irrigation,
decorative boulders, a new drinking fountain, benches, fencing, a dedicated
dog area, and ADA improvements. The area of work is along Adeline Street,
from the intersection with MLK Jr Way toward Alcatraz Ave. Approximate
area of improvements is highlighted in red on the map.

Willard Clubhouse Public Art Unveiling (Follow
Up)




New Willard Clubhouse Mosaic Art - Enchanted Forest


If you missed the unveiling of the new artwork at the Willard Clubhouse on
February 11th, you need to visit and view the Enchanted Forest, by artist
Julie W. Chang, a mural that draws inspiration from Berkeley’s flora and
fauna and the history of Willard Park as a beloved gathering space.




                                               3
The unveiling was paired with an exciting milestone for the building, making
this a celebration of both art and environmental stewardship. As the City of
Berkeley’s first all-electric municipal building, the Willard Clubhouse
exceeded its design goal of LEED Silver and achieved LEED Gold
certification for its energy efficiency, reduced environmental impact, and
commitment to occupant health and comfort.

This welcoming community space is a new all-electric milestone for
Berkeley’s portfolio of sustainably designed municipal buildings. The project
uses on-site solar power and battery storage to support the city's climate
action goals and has been honored with an award for Outstanding Facility
by the California Parks and Recreation Society’s District 3. Congratulations!

PRW Capital Projects Team




Completed Capital Projects


The City of Berkeley manages Capital Improvement Projects (CIP) across
the city to modernize aging facilities, improve accessibility, meet current
safety standards, and support evolving community needs. Investments in
park and facility improvements help strengthen recreational programming,
protect neighborhood vitality, and build our shared social infrastructure.

The Department of Parks, Recreation & Waterfront (PRW) leads its own
Capital Improvement Division, made up of three working groups overseeing

                                      4
 projects throughout Berkeley and at the City’s residential camps in
 Tuolumne, Sonoma, and El Dorado counties. PRW projects support parks,
 recreation centers, pools, sports fields, public restrooms, open spaces,
 camps, and the Berkeley Marina and Waterfront.
