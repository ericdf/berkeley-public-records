           Ribbon Cutting for the South Adeline
           Improvements: Dog Park, Landscaping
           Improvements and Miyawaki Forest
           On Saturday, June 20, 2026, the City of Berkeley celebrated the opening of
           a new dog park, a Miyawaki forest, and new landscaping improvements at
           Adeline Street at MLK Jr. Way near the Oakland border! The Miyawaki
           Forest was designed to address urban heat islands and biodiversity using
           dense planting, while the landscaping improvements add beauty, color,
           texture, and add to the City’s pollinator corridor. The dog park provides a
           new amenity to an underutilized space and activates the area for
           community use. Together, these improvements improve safety, create
           more green space, and add amenities to an area which has historically
           lacked appropriate park and open spaces. We are excited to share these
           improvements in South Berkeley!

MLK Jr. Youth Services Center YAP Renovation and Seismic Upgrade


The Martin Luther King Jr. Youth Services Center | Young Adult Project
(Y.A.P.) is a well‐used 10,655 square foot community center adjacent to
Grove Park at 1730 Oregon Street. The building is one of the City’s 5
community centers operated by the Parks, Recreation & Waterfront (PRW)
Department, and one of the City’s designated care and shelter facilities
identified in the City’s Resilience Strategy, Emergency Operations Plan, and
Local Hazard Mitigation Plan. As a community center, the PRW Department
provides recreational programming and services for residents of all ages

                                              3
here, but this is the only community center with a particular focus on young
adults and teens. The primary objective of this project is to create a more
modern and flexible recreation center that is safe and accessible to the local
teens and community it serves. The project includes:

      An interior remodel to re‐size program spaces, for flexibility in future
       recreational programming opportunities,
      An improved community room, a new teen lounge, computer lab and
       a new recording studio,
      A seismic retrofit of the 1950’s building to upgrade the building to an
       Immediate Occupancy level,
      Improvements to fire and life safety systems,
      Upgrades the building to incorporate universal design and increase
       accessibility, and
      Will meet the City of Berkeley’s sustainability goals with full
       electrification, passive design strategies, and healthy building
       materials.

Construction is progressing for the renovation and expansion of MLK Jr.
Youth Services Center, and currently:

      Extensive dry rot was replaced.
      Framing is complete, and roofing installation has begun.
      Exterior waterproofing has started.
      Electrical, plumbing, and other utility work is underway.

Baring any unforeseen circumstances, we are well on our way to re-opening
in early 2027!

PRW Capital Projects Team
The City of Berkeley manages Capital Improvement Projects (CIP) across the
city to modernize aging facilities, improve accessibility, meet current safety
standards, and support evolving community needs. Investments in park and
facility improvements help strengthen recreational programming, protect
neighborhood vitality, and build our shared social infrastructure.

The Department of Parks, Recreation & Waterfront (PRW) leads its own Capital
Improvement Division, made up of three working groups overseeing projects
throughout Berkeley and at the City’s residential camps in Tuolumne, Sonoma,
and El Dorado counties. PRW projects support parks, recreation centers, pools,
sports fields, public restrooms, open spaces, camps, and the Berkeley Marina
and Waterfront.
