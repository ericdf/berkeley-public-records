                Berkeley Pier/Ferry Feasibility Study – Questionnaire #1 Results Summary



                 Online Questionnaire #1 – Results Report
                      City of Berkeley Department of Parks, Recreation and Waterfront
                      San Francisco Water Emergency Transportation Authority (WETA)
                                             October 21, 2021


This report summarizes the results of the Online Questionnaire #1 for the Berkeley/WETA Pier and
Ferry Feasibility Study public engagement process. The questionnaire was posted on the City’s
Pier/Ferry project website between March 22nd and June 6th and was open to anyone interested in
the project including non-Berkeley residents. A total of (377) responses were received of whom
92% reside in Berkeley and adjacent communities.

The questionnaire consisted of eleven questions: six on ferry use and operations, two on pier
design, and three on the respondent’s demographic information. Four of the eleven questions
allowed for an optional open-ended “Other” response for written comments. The City/WETA
project team designed the questionnaire and hosted it on the SurveyMonkey platform.

This report includes: 1) overall key findings and a summary of each question’s response data; and,
2) an appendix of the chart data and a compilation of comments for the open-ended questions. The
raw source data is available upon request.


Key Findings
The following findings comprise most, though not all, of the key themes from the (377) responses.

1. There is strong interest to commute and pleasure travel to San Francisco and pleasure travel to
   Marin County. There is interest to commute to other destinations.
2. Respondents would use transit and bicycles to access the ferry terminal if such options and
   infrastructure were offered.
3. People strongly support restoration of the existing or development of a new recreation-focused
   pier with amenities and programming, such as: tables/benches; viewing spots; programming to
   foster cultural activity and small-scale events/performances; interpretive information on
   habitat, local and Indigenous Peoples history; design amenities to support walking/strolling
   and a kid-friendly environment; food and beverage services; small-scale retail; fishing; water
   recreation suitable for a pier; and a safe, clean, well-lit and regularly patrolled environment.
4. For the overall project, people expressed with high frequency the desire for:
   – mitigating potential traffic and parking impacts from new ferry commuter travel;
   – using existing parking (e.g. Hs Lordship’s lot) for multiple uses; and,
   – providing launch point(s) for kayak, windsurf, small watercraft, swimming.
5. Some people expressed a preference for no ferry service where more passive, low-intensive use
   — nature paths, open space, limited commercial activity — would be encouraged.
