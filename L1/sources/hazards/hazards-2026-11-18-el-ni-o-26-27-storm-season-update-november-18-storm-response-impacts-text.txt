Office of the City Manager




November 18, 2026


To:              Honorable Mayor and Members of the City Council
From:            Paul Buddenhagen, City Manager
Re:              El Niño 26-27 Storm Season Update: November 18 Storm Response &
                    Impacts

Today’s heavy rain and strong winds caused significant flooding, fallen trees, power
outages, and water intrusion at businesses and City facilities. By the mid-afternoon,
Berkeley had received 2.54 inches of rain. Throughout the day, City staff worked
through the EOC structure and in the field with Caltrans, CHP, and PG&E to address
immediate hazards, assist people affected by flooding, and maintain City services.

Key activities and impacts are summarized below.

Response Staffing and Public Assistance
Public Works had received 100 calls for service by 1:00 p.m. and reported distributing
more than 400 sandbags to Berkeley community members during the day. Fire
department personnel normally assigned to administrative duties were moved into field
response roles to help manage increased demand and prioritize incidents requiring an
immediate emergency response.

Ashby Underpass
The Ashby underpass (leading to and from I-80) flooded, leading to Fire personnel
crews rescued two people stuck there; a third person self-rescued after firefighters
arrived. The Ashby underpass eastbound and westbound I-80 offramps at Ashby
remained closed at the latest update. Public Works and Caltrans were working to
remove water, with CHP also on scene.

West Berkeley Flooding
Businesses on both sides of the railroad tracks near Gilman Street experienced
extensive flooding, requiring road closures, Public Works response, and towing of a
flooded vehicle. Water was reported receding around noon, and Gilman Street between
Fourth Street and west of Third Street reopened during the afternoon.

Codornices Creek flooding affected Second Street. The Transfer Station remained
operational despite flooding at its entrance and Equipment Maintenance Shop. The
Recycling Center did not flood.

Aquatic Park and Bolivar Drive flooded. No flooding was reported inside the four
identified City tenant buildings along Bolivar Drive, although they remained a concern.

At the Waterfront, several DoubleTree Hotel rooms flooded from water backing up
through a storm drain. At the Marina, waves overtopped the South Cove seawall during
the morning’s peak winds, and staff secured two boats whose dock lines had broken.

Trees and power outages
Forestry crews responded to fallen City and private trees and branches throughout
Berkeley. By 3:45 p.m., PRW-Forestry reported that the identified tree hazards had
been made safe and affected public rights of way reopened. Nearly 200 PG&E
customers near Ashby Avenue between Acton Street and San Pablo Avenue were
without power, with additional smaller outages elsewhere.

City Facilities
Portions of Fire Headquarters flooded, including the east parking area and offices and
conference rooms along the eastern side of the building. Most standing water in the
parking area had been pumped out by the afternoon. Drain investigation, carpet
removal, and drying were underway. Public Works also responded to roof leaks and
drainage problems at several other City facilities, including the City Attorney’s Office,
City Manager’s side of the 5th floor at 2180 Milvia and Animal Shelter.

Public Messaging
Police issued a Nixle alert directing people away from the flooded Ashby area, Alameda
County distributed flood warning information through AC Alert, and City
Communications shared winter preparedness information and guidance on reporting
storm problems.

These reports provide a preliminary picture. The full extent of damage and service
impacts is still being assessed. Additional updates will be forthcoming as conditions
change and more is known.
