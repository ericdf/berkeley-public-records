                                     Overview and Purpose

The City Charter requires the City Manager to present an annual estimate of the upcoming
year’s financial needs and that the Council adopt an Annual Appropriations Ordinance that
sets expenditure limits by fund. For Fiscal Year (FY) 2027 and 2028, the City will again
develop a Biennial Budget.

The City of Berkeley’s Biennial Budget is an important document. It reflects not only our financial
plan for the next two fiscal years, but also our community’s priorities, Council’s adopted policies,
and the City’s strategy for balancing fiscal responsibility with high-quality and varied services.

This cycle presents significant challenges. The City faces a projected structural General Fund
deficit that is currently estimated at $28 million in FY 2027 and $29 million in FY 2028, with
potential increases following baseline adjustments. Additional pressures include:

   •   Rising Pension and Healthcare costs

   •   Upcoming Labor Contract Negotiations

   •   Escalating Operating Expenses (e.g., Insurance, Utilities)

   •   Inflation

   •   Shortfalls in enterprise and other funds (e.g., Marina, Parking, Building Maintenance)

   •   Behavioral Health Services Act Transition

   •   Delayed Facility Maintenance

   •   Unfunded/Underfunded Liabilities

   •   Uncertainty in Federal Funding

One-time solutions deployed in prior fiscal years to balance the City’s budget are not sustainable;
therefore, this budget cycle prioritizes aligning ongoing expenditures with ongoing revenues.

To meet these challenges head-on, the City is launching the budget process earlier than in
previous years. Budget instructions, typically released in January, are now being issued in
October to allow more time for strategic analysis, innovation, and refinement. It reflects the desire
to provide an open and extensive discussion of the budget and greater opportunity for input.

The goal is to submit a Biennial Budget that:
   • Reflects realistic baseline costs and revenues.
   • Identifies sustainable reductions and operational efficiencies.
   • Proposes new or adjusted revenue opportunities.
   • Aligns with the City’s Strategic Plan and Council priorities.
   • Builds trust through clear communication and documentation.

 The City Manager will present a proposed balanced budget to Council on May 12, 2026.
 Council will adopt the two-year budget on June 30, 2026, along with the Annual
 Appropriations Ordinance for FY 2027.

                    Council Fiscal Policies and Budget Development

The City Council’s adopted fiscal policies set the framework for the development of the
upcoming budget and should be used as a guide when developing the FY 2027 & 2028
budget. These policies include:
    1. Focusing on the long-term fiscal health of the City by adopting a two-year budget and
        conducting multi-year planning.
    2. Building prudent reserves with a target range of between 20 to 30% of General Fund
        revenues.
    3. Developing long-term strategies to reduce unfunded liabilities.
    4. Controlling labor costs while minimizing layoffs and promoting retention of City staff.
    5. Primarily allocating one-time revenue for one-time expenditures (e.g., capital investments
        and deferred maintenance).
    6. Requiring enterprise and grant funds to balance and new programs to pay for themselves.
    7. Any new expenditure requires new revenue or expenditure reductions.
    8. Property transfer tax:
            a. When possible, allocate excess property transfer tax over the baseline to short-
                  term operational needs, General Fund reserves and the City’s capital
                  infrastructure plan.
            b. Increase the property transfer tax baseline for operational needs with a permanent
                  adjustment to $16.0 million beginning in fiscal year 2025
    9. As the General Fund subsidy to the Safety Members Pension fund declines over the next
        several years, the amount of the annual decrease will be used to help fund the new Police
        Employee Retiree Health Plan.
    10. Allocating annual savings derived from the prepayment of the annual CalPERS unfunded
        liability payments to the City’s Section 115 Pension Trust up to $5.5 million per fiscal year
        or as advised by the City’s actuarial and staff.
    11. Allocating any additional revenue earned from investments that is over the annual (fiscal
        year) baseline of $6 million in the following manner:
            a. One-third to the Section 115 Pension Trust up to $5.5 million per fiscal year, or as
                  advised by the City’s actuarial and staff,
            b. One-third to General Fund reserves up to 30% of General Fund revenue, or as
                  recommended by Council policy, and
            c. One-third to address the City’s capital infrastructure plan.

                           Assess Revenue and Funding Sources

In previous budget developments, staff have focused more towards refining the expenditure side
of the budget, ensuring funds are available to support planned costs. However, effective
budgeting begins with a clear understanding of available revenues, as well. Understanding
the funding sources available, developing proper forecasting, looking at revenue opportunities,
fees, and cost recovery, and updating projected revenues helps allocate resources wisely and
avoid financial shortfalls, which often leads to extensive use of fund balances and reliance on
discretionary revenue sources, such as the General Fund. Proper planning ensures that essential
services are funded, and financial stability is maintained.

Funds
During this budget cycle, the first round of revenue projections using FY 2026 first quarter actuals.
will be used for the initial review and refinement of departmental budget occurring from October
2025 to November 2025.

The second round of revenue projections using FY 2026 second quarter actuals will be used
during the department development of budget balancing strategies in January and February 2026.

The Budget Office is working with the Finance Department on updating General Fund revenue
projections for FY 2027 & 2028. Departments must analyze trends for all baseline revenues and
revise estimates for the funds they manage. The Budget Office and Finance Department will
support departments and Fund Managers to ensure revenue estimates are accurate and
verifiable.

For any fund projecting a deficit, departments must submit a structural balancing plan. This
includes sustainable revenue increases and/or expenditure reductions that do not impact the
General Fund. Special fund deficits create direct liabilities for the General Fund; therefore,
balancing solutions must be implemented without shifting burdens to other departments or
citywide resources. The status of special funds will be reviewed during departmental meetings
with the City Manager.

Fees
As part of the City’s strategy to strengthen revenues and maintain cost recovery, departments
should evaluate current fees and propose adjustments where appropriate. Fee proposals will be
reviewed in alignment with citywide policy to ensure fees and taxes reflect the full cost of service
delivery.

   •   Fee Increase Report Submittal Guidance: January 2026
   •   Fee Information due to Budget Office: February 2026
   •   Public Hearing on Fee updates: May 2026

Grants
Grants play a critical role in the City’s budget by bridging funding gaps and advancing unfunded
initiatives. However, departments must carefully assess the long-term viability of grant-funded
programs. Consider:
     • Sustainability after the grant ends, especially if staffing or service expectations are built
         around it.
     • Administrative burden on existing staff and compliance costs
     • Matching fund requirements, which may strain the General Fund or divert resources from
         other priorities.

                              FY 2027 & FY 2028 Baseline Budget

The Baseline Budget Review

Historically, the City’s baseline budget starts with the prior year’s adopted budget, excluding one-
time allocations. For FY 2027 & FY 2028, our goal is to align budgets with actual
expenditures, eliminate inefficiencies, stop capturing outdated budgets and spending
patterns. The goal is to ensure the budget that is adopted reflects true service costs and strategic
priorities. A more accurate baseline reduces adjustments during the fiscal year (AAO#1 and
AAO#2) and limits the interruption in projects, programs, and services.

Key Phases of Baseline Review

October 2025 and November 2025
   • Budget Office captures live payroll snapshot and generates Salary & Benefits projections.
   • Personnel Review: Departments review Position Control (saved in Teams Channel) and
      submits changes to Budget; Budget processes corrections in Salary and Benefits
      Projection

Any corrections or edits to these reports should be submitted to the Budget Office using the forms
provided in the ERMA Budget Module Instructions and saved in Team Channel If there is a
change that needs to be made to live Position Control, departments will need to contact Human
Resources to make those corrections. The Budget Office can only make corrections to the salary
and benefit projections and not live Position Control.

Additional steps have been added to this biennial budget process before the Budget Office
develops the baseline budget.

November 2025
   • Non-Personnel Review: Departments update FY 2027 & FY 2028 operating budgets based
     on actuals. The Budget Office will provide the General Fund template to be used for this
     exercise and will be saved in the Teams Channel. Departments must justify any amount
     exceeding the Budget Office amounts. Due November 26, 2026.
   • Other funds that are projecting deficits must submit similar templates that are used in the
     General Fund review and submit it to Budget Office. Due November 26, 2026.
   • Departments should do the same analysis for all funds that make up their budget and
     should be used to update when Department Entry Level is open in ERMA.
   • Revenue Review: Departments assess and update revenue projections (see “Assess
     Revenue and Funding Sources”).

December 2025
   • Budget Office develops baseline budget incorporating outcomes from October and
     November exercise.
   • Initial baseline budgets and Salary & Benefits projections are reviewed collaboratively with
     departments.

It is important to remember that the baseline budget is a starting point to determine the costs to
continue current levels of service. It is not a guarantee to continue funding at this level. In fact, due
to the challenges already mentioned in this document, significant reductions are anticipated.

Baseline Expenditure Assumptions

Personnel Expenditures
The personnel budgets are built using the current personnel costs as reflected in live payroll (as of
October 16, 2025), adjusted to include known equity and cost of living adjustments per current
labor contracts, as well as updated benefit rates. Benefits reflect (1) projected increases in health
and dental costs; (2) estimated CalPERS retirement rate contributions; (3) revised Retiree Medical
benefit rates; (4) revised Workers’ Compensation rates; and (5) other benefits and add pays.

As these amounts are calculated using the ERMA Salary & Benefit Projections, departments will
not be able to change personnel-related items (object range 511110 – 530110) with the following
exceptions allowed for non-general fund:

       Hourly Salaries (512110 and 512120)

       Overtime expenditures (513110 and 513140)

As a reminder, the baseline budget includes the continuation of not funding 44.4 vacant
positions identified in the FY 2026 Adopted budget. Any adjustments to salary savings will
be made by the Budget Office pending direction from the City Manager.

Non-Personnel Costs
The non-personnel baseline of the General Fund and other funds projecting deficits will already be
updated to reflect the genuine cost of service by using historical actuals and not prior year
adopted budget and incremental budgeting.

Non-personnel budget of all other funds should be updated following the template used to review
and update the General Fund baseline. This can be done in the Budget Entry Level in ERMA.

The annual CIP Fund (Fund 501) allocation is also included. However, all other capital funding will
be incorporated during the Department Entry Level and Department Head Level as we build the
City’s 5-Year CIP.

Community Agencies
To preserve funding allocated to Community Agency Grants, object codes 636110 and 636990
are no longer part of the rollup code. Changes to these accounts will be done by HHCS and
Budget Office.

Internal Service Charges and Indirect Charges
Departments will not be able to adjust internal service charges (Object Range 670100 – 670240)
as part of the baseline budget. Internal Service funds recover their operating costs through
charges to departments for specific services. The FY 2027 and FY 2028 baseline internal service
will reflect the FY 2026 Adopted Budget. The charges may change after the budget balancing
exercise.

The internal service charges include:

   •   Facility Maintenance Charges: All departments housed in City-owned facilities are
       assessed a facilities maintenance charge. These assessments cover the costs for
       janitorial, building maintenance and property management needs at all City facilities,
       including departments occupying leased office space.

   •   Equipment Maintenance & Replacement: These charges are for maintenance and
       replacement of fleet vehicles and capital equipment.

   •   Information Technology Service Fund: This fund was set up to systematically modernize
       Citywide Technology infrastructure and safeguard the efficiency of our technology assets
       such as network infrastructure, network operations, enterprise services, database and GIS
       services, helpdesk, implementation, and ongoing support of technology applications. Each
       department is required to contribute based on annual budget times a ratio, which is the
       number of existing PCs within the department for hardware and based on the number of

   •   Central Services Fund: This internal service fund provides mail services to the entire City,
       including the pickup of incoming United States Postal Service mail, and the sorting and
       delivery of both USPS mail and interoffice mail to individual departments.

Indirect Costs
Indirect costs represent General Fund expenditures incurred by the City’s support departments.
Historically, these costs have been charged to Non-General Fund activities to reimburse the
General Fund for costs incurred in support of these activities. While the City’s current indirect cost
plan reflects cost recovery rates for all departments and funds, only specific non-General Fund
departments are assessed. Indirect cost amounts charged to Departments will be updated and
reflected in ERMA object code 670210. Departments cannot change these accounts.

The Budget Office will periodically adjust these accounts to reflect changes in baseline personnel
labor cost distributions. FY 2027 & FY 2028 baseline expects 22% to Parks, Recreation and
Waterfront and Public Works, respectively and 24% to Planning. The Budget Office will update,
and the percentage could change for FY 2027 and FY 2028 based on the FY 2024 published
Indirect Cost Allocation Plan rates. The affected departments will be notified. Adjustments to the
published IDC rates must be approved by the Finance Director and Budget Manager.

Debt
Debt service schedules should be reviewed to identify principal and interest expenditures for the
upcoming fiscal year, ensuring all debt service costs are accurately reflected in the budget. The
Budget Office, in coordination with Finance, will update Citywide debt service payments for FY
2027 and FY 2028, incorporating them into the baseline budget. However, departments are
responsible for verifying that debt service payments related to their own obligations are updated
correctly. This should be completed during the Budget Entry Level process in ERMA.

Transfers
Budget Office will review and update the Annual Transfers list.

                     Balancing the FY 2027 & FY 2028 Biennial Budget

As previously noted, the City faces significant fiscal challenges in this biennial budget cycle, with a
projected structural General Fund deficit that are currently estimated at $28 million in FY 2027 and
$29 million in FY 2028, figures that may grow following baseline adjustments. Balancing rising
expenditures against limited revenues remains a difficult task. With this in mind, once the baseline
budget is developed, the City will move into budget balancing phase.

Some key dates and deliverables for this phase are:

January 2026
   • Budget Office provides updated FY 2027 and FY 2028 surplus/deficit estimates based on
      updated baseline and General Fund revenue projection.
   • Departments must prepare General Fund reduction plans equal to 10% of their budgets,
      net of any revenue loss associated with impacted positions.
   • Departments managing other funds that are projecting deficits must submit reduction plans
      sufficient to fully balance each fund in FY 2027. Target reductions will vary based on the
      size of each fund’s projected shortfall.
   • All departments submit revenue proposals and preliminary balancing strategies.

Budget Balancing Measures templates will be available on the Teams Channel.

February 2026
Departmental Meetings with the City Manager. Agenda will include:
   • FY 2027–2028 baseline analysis
   • General Fund reduction plans and balancing strategies
   • New mandates and fee updates (if applicable)
   • Special fund forecasts and capital projects
   • Current and emerging fiscal challenges

March 2026
   • Refine projections based on departmental strategies and City Manager meetings.
   • Begin discussions with the Budget & Finance Policy Committee.
   • Finalize Proposed Budget Recommendations.

April 2026 – June 2026
   • Develop the Proposed Biennial Budget
   • Continue engagement with stakeholders: CMO, staff, labor groups, Budget & Finance
        Committee, Council, and the community.
   • Adopt a balanced FY 2027 & FY 2028 Biennial Budget

Other important factors happening throughout Budget Development
   • Engage bargaining groups and employees for input and collaboration.
   • Identify and implement operational efficiencies that yield real savings.
   • Inventory City-owned land and properties:
          o Evaluate opportunities to relocate programs from external leased spaces to City-
              owned facilities.
          o Assess potential land dispositions that could generate one-time resources
              Lead: Public Works, in collaboration with CMO and Finance.

                           Budget Development & ERMA Budget Module


 Purpose
 The ERMA Budget Module will maximize the integration with the City’s financial systems and
 provide greater control over elements of the budget process.
 ERMA Budget Levels and Budget Entry Procedures
 The Budget Module consists of a series of Budget “Levels” as follows:
      ▪   Baseline Budget Level: Created by the Budget Office, and is adjusted for known and
          estimated salary and benefit costs. If there are any known one-time items at this early
          stage of the budget process, those will be included in the baseline budget.
      ▪   Department Entry Level Budget: This is the level where Departments may modify their
          respective baseline budgets by moving appropriations among various spending categories.
          Departments may increase or decrease their budget by account codes. The City
          Manager’s approval is required before departments change their General Fund baseline
          budget.
      ▪   Department Head Budget Level: This level allows Departments to modify their respective
          budgets under certain conditions such as direction from the City Manager, more refined
          Capital budget, new revenues, and expenditures stemming from previously unknown
          mandates or projects. Personnel changes requested during the Department Entry Budget
          Level should be reflected at this level.
      ▪   Proposed Budget Level: Departments can no longer make entries/changes at this Budget
          level. Only the Budget Office can work at this level. Only changes initiated by the City
          Manager’s Office are allowed at this level.
      ▪   Adopted Budget Level: This is the final level of the Budget Development process. There
          should be no more changes occurring at this level.
 Departments will modify their respective baseline budgets by moving appropriations among
 various spending categories within the Departmental Entry Level Budget. Departments may
 increase or decrease their budget by account codes. Departments cannot change their
 General Fund baseline budget. Any changes will be made by the Budget Office.

There are two methods for entering budget requests into the Central Budget Entry as follows:

 1.       Amount Entry: A flat dollar amount is entered as an account’s requested budget.

 2.    Detail Entry (Preferred method): Detail lines are added for each account, and the
 requested budget reflects the total of all the detail lines.

 Department should refer to the ERMA Central Budget Entry Instructions for guidance in
 developing their budgets in ERMA and may also contact the Budget Office for assistance.

 Departmental users are authorized to access selected levels to complete their budget
 submissions. Each Budget Level has an access start and end date meaning that once the
 level(s) reaches its end date, Departments can no longer make changes.

     •   Organizational Chart

The Strategic Plan

As part of the FY 2027 & FY 2028 budget development process, departments will include
Strategic Plan projects and programs in the department’s section of the Budget Book.
These projects and programs are both items carried over from the previous budget cycle (i.e.,
those that are not expected to be completed by June 30, 2026) and any new projects and
programs proposed for the next two-year budget cycle. This will constitute the Strategic Plan
update, which is a component of the biennial budget process.
To be considered a Strategic Plan project or program, it must:
   ▪ Be a specific two-year project, program, or phase of a project or program designed to
       advance one of the strategic plan’s nine goals.
   ▪ Have the budget to conduct the project or program (or a clear phase of it) within two
       years.
   ▪ Have a clear scope of work and timeline.
   ▪ Be in addition to “baseline services” departments provide.
In November 2020, departments participated in an exercise identifying a Priority List of items
based on Council discussion of its priorities in October. Departments should remove any items
from this list which both (1) originated as referrals and (2) are not yet underway. These items will
be considered as part of Council ranking of referrals (i.e., re-weighted range voting [RRV]).
Departments should use remaining items on the Priority List as the basis of their Strategic Plan
projects and programs for FY 2027 & FY 2028.
For each project or program, indicate:
   ▪ Strategic Plan Goal which it supports,
   ▪ Supporting Departments,
   ▪ Title,
   ▪ Description,
   ▪ Planned start and end dates,
   ▪ Whether it’s a new or continuing item, and
   ▪ Estimated budget

Performance Measures

Performance measures will continue to be a part of the Strategic Plan and the budget process. In
terms of the budget process, performance measures will be reported initially in the departmental
presentations to the Budget & Finance Policy Committee and in the Budget Book. In the Budget
