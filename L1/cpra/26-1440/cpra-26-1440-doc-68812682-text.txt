                                 The Fiscal Gap
The Department of Information Technology is facing a structural deficit
that cannot be resolved through standard efficiency measures. Our
baseline expenditures are dictated by fixed personnel costs and non-
negotiable software contracts essential to City operations.

                                        FY2027                   FY2028
    Total Expenditure                   $18,597,742.95**         $18,932,622.95**
    Proposed Revenue Target:            $15,272,055.00*          $15,272,055.00*

            Our Revenue Stream: General Fund
IT’s main source of active revenue comes solely from the Internal Service
Fund 680 (Cost Allocation). The only other “significant” fund source is the
General Fund (011) that accounts for only about 7 percent of IT’s total
revenues, or about $1.2M.




We have completed the modeling for the requested 10% and 12.5%
General Fund reductions. The actual budgetary impact results in a net
savings of $126,391 (11%) in the first scenario and $185,391 (16%) in the
second.

                    Impact of Historical Actions
It is vital to provide context on our starting position. In 2025, $6,466,414.23 was
removed from the IT Cost Allocation budget in a one-time sweep.

This represents the "stability reserve" that would have mitigated the current
shortfall and by removing this revenue, the department no longer has a "buffer" to
absorb the revenue reductions proposed by the Budget Office. We are now being
asked to cut operational "muscle" because our fiscal "fat" was already harvested.

        Our Revenue Stream: Cost Allocation (680)
The IT Cost Allocation (Fund 680) is funded through a complex formula that yields an
annual per head cost. This value is $10,628.30 but has not been revised in several
years to account for three critical "Cost Escalators“:

1. Annual Vendor Inflation: Enterprise software vendors implement on average,
   12–15% price increases upon renewal. The Impact: Our fixed-cost contracts
   are expiring; staying at the current baseline is effectively a service reduction.

2. Unfunded Regulatory Mandates: Cybersecurity requirements (CJIS, HIPAA,
   and insurance compliance) necessitate immediate infrastructure upgrades. The
   Impact: These are non-discretionary costs—meeting these mandates is required
   to maintain city operations and eligibility for insurance.

3. Cumulative Technical Debt: Deferring updates will create a "maintenance
   backlog.“ The Impact: We will be managing aging equipment that is more
   expensive to repair and maintain than to replace. The $10.6k baseline assumes
   "normal" operations, not the deferred maintenance recovery that will be required
   as a result of cuts.

                       The Pain of the Exercise
IT will reluctantly provide a 10% reduction plan based on current CBE
figures. However, we must formally advise that cutting from an already
understated revenue baseline does not yield true savings. Here’s why:

    • Benchmarking cuts against inaccurate starting figures creates a
      structural deficit. We are not "saving" money; we are deferring
      essential costs.

    • Compounding Interest: Underfunding our $10,628.30 baseline
      results in "Technical Debt" that will inevitably come due as
      system failures or security breaches.

    • Emergency fixes in the SF Bay Area are 3x to 5x more
      expensive than planned maintenance.*

    *Industry Benchmarks (Siemens, US DOE): Reactive maintenance costs average 3–5x more
    than preventive measures.

                                 Conclusion
On paper, our cuts will reach the Budget Manager’s target. But the reality is that we
have 'balanced the books' by selling our insurance policy and firing the fire
department (to use an analogy).

We have zero resilience: No reserves, Loss of permanent FTEs, Moving from
Proactive Maintenance to "Hope-Based" Operations.

Between the 2025 $6.4M sweep and these proposed cuts, the IT Department is
moving from a 'Modernization' posture to one of 'Fragile Survival.’ We will be slower
to respond, unable to innovate, and significantly more vulnerable to a catastrophic
cyber event and/or infrastructure failure and an inability to address them.

Our resource pool (knowledge base) consist of contractors who are beyond their
retirement years and may be not be available any time soon. We have already lost
two FTE who had institutional knowledge of FUND$. We need to modernize
quickly.

We are prepared to move forward if mandated, but the City must accept the
heightened risk to operational continuity.
